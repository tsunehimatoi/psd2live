# Deformer and parameter reference

[Documentation](../README.md) · [PSD artwork and naming](PSD_LAYER_SPEC.md)

This page describes the default structure that automatic generation produces, and the editing conventions. The actual objects depend on the artwork, the model preset, and later edits. Use the hierarchy tree, the parameter panel, or the value returned by MCP `workspace_inspect`.

## Structure

The body's root is `DeformBodyXY`. It covers only the body's own parts (torso, clothes, arms, skirt, tail) and the neck that turns with the head. It does not cover hair or the legs. Body X turns the upper body above the waist as one piece around the center line. The waist and the chest turn by the same amount, and the turn fades out at the hips: the chest moves toward the turn side, the shoulder that comes forward is slightly larger and slightly lower, the neck stays near the axis and barely moves, and the head stays in the middle of the body. Body Y opens the shoulders slightly when it goes down and straightens the back when it goes up. The body also moves with the hips: on Body X it shifts slightly sideways; when Body Y goes down it sinks and the knees draw inward, and when Body Y goes up the legs straighten and rise slightly. Under it, in order, are `DeformBodyLean` (two parameters. “Body lean”: the body leans forward in three dimensions as a solid with thickness and is drawn in perspective. The pelvis leans forward part of the way about the hip joint and carries the skirt. The spine bends gradually along an arc at the waist until it reaches the full angle, and the waist is not squashed on its own. While leaning forward the chest shortens in perspective, the whole body moves down and grows slightly, the head keeps its shape and moves down with the neck, and the face turns downward at the same time. Leaning back makes it smaller and lifts the face slightly. “Proportion (chibi)”: a positive value enlarges the whole head from the neck, widens the chest, shortens the torso and the arms, and shortens the legs and the skirt. The upper body moves down with that, closer to a chibi proportion. A negative value goes the other way, closer to a normal proportion. The feet do not move. When the skeleton is off, each arm also has a `DeformArmHang_L/R` whose axes are Body X and these two parameters. The arm moves and scales with the shoulder as one piece hanging from the shoulder, and does not bend with the body. On Body X the arm does not grow and drop toward the turn side; it swings about 4° the other way about the shoulder, and the hand lags the body slightly. When the skeleton is on, the upper-arm rotation deformer likewise uses Body X to cancel the inherited turn and makes the same swing) and `DeformBodyZBreath` (Body Z / breath). Head rotation and head-shell follow sit under that. A standing full-body character also has a sibling root `DeformLegs` (the legs, with axes Body X, Body Y, and Proportion). It carries only the legs and the shoes. The feet do not move under any body parameter. The face latitude-longitude mesh, the face outline, and the feature offsets take over the eyes, brows, nose, mouth, and ears. Front hair and back hair each have a head-shell follow and a physics branch. The actual hierarchy also contains region groups, pupil hold / gaze, and optional lips and other objects. The concrete structure is whatever the hierarchy tree or MCP `workspace_inspect` returns. The amount of the generated rig at a parameter's full value can be adjusted in Values on each part card of the model preset, or in MCP `settings.rigTuning`: head turn and the face plane; the features (eyes, pupils, gaze, eye close, brows, nose, mouth, ears); hair follow and swing; the nod and the lift while leaning forward and back; and each body parameter (turn angle, arm swing, hip shift, sink and rise, knees, back and shoulders, lean angle and how much the pelvis shares, each proportion item, Body Z and breath, torso thickness and viewing distance). Uncommon items are folded into Advanced inside that card. When a module or a base algorithm draws a part itself (stylized head turn, fitted eyes, brows, and mouth, hair chains), the corresponding value is hidden. The defaults are the amounts described above. Head range and Body range then scale the head and the body as a whole.

## Coordinates and keyforms

The canvas origin is at the top left. X goes right and Y goes down. Object geometry is evaluated in the parent's local space. A Warp's normalized local coordinates and a Rotation's local coordinates cannot be mixed. Rest meshes and keyforms also use different coordinate conventions during conversion. Export handles that through conversions such as restMeshesToCanvasSpace.

Direct binding decides the shape axes of the object itself. Parent parameters are inherited through the hierarchy. A keyform is the object's shape at a parameter coordinate, not an animation time frame. Animation files drive parameters over time, and physics also outputs parameter values. After a parameter is added, a shape still has to be bound before there is motion.

## Automatic deformation

Head X/Y builds nine poses from the combination of endpoints and the midpoint. The head tilt in the original art is estimated as the local base. The face uses a latitude-longitude mesh and regional reshaping. Eyes and brows, pupils, the mouth, and the ears have their own compensation or mask logic. The legs bend with two-bone IK through hip, knee, and ankle. The knee bends toward the viewer. From the front the thigh shortens instead of swinging outward. Legs drawn on one layer can still bend at each knee. Head rotation, and bone rotation deformers parented directly to the body, also scale with body lean and proportion as extra axes. When the legs are split and bound to leg bones, each leg bone that carries a leg mesh has another standing-pose warp whose axes are Body X/Y. It places the mesh where the leg warp puts it: a rotation deformer passes only the pivot and the angle, so the bend of the leg warp does not reach below it. Planar tilt and breath are layered separately. Body Z tilts everything above the waist line about the waist. Each row only translates, and the width stays the same. Breath is placed from the shoulder line and the waist line in the original art. It lifts the shoulders and the head and arms they carry, the chest expands slightly, and everything below the waist stays still. Front hair and back hair are separated from strong face deformation, and each outputs a swing parameter. Eye deformation can be driven by blink physics.

**Brows and eyes through bangs** (`SeeThroughLayers`): Each eyebrow covered by front hair gets a copy, drawn above the bangs that cover it, shown semi-transparent, and clipped by those bangs meshes so it appears only inside the bangs. This is the usual approach in anime-style Live2D models: copy the eyebrow layer, raise it above the bangs, lower the opacity, and clip with the bangs. It happens in two steps. The base rig only records candidates (`BuiltRig.seeThrough`: each eyebrow or each eye layer — eye white, iris including highlights, eyelashes, and the closed-eye line — and the bangs meshes that cover it by pixels in the rest pose, saved with the materialized rig regardless of the settings. The save includes `see_through_rules`. Candidates that 3.6.5 saved under the old rule, which recorded only eyelashes and the closed-eye line, are read as unknown, and the preview regenerates them). The finished rig makes the copies after the journal, the generators, the generated-result overrides, layer visibility, and draw-order overrides, according to the settings (`BuiltRig.seeThroughCopies` records each copy against its original. Only that record is recognized. Copies are not identified by ID). A copy's ID is `<原件 ID>_SeeThrough`. If a mesh of that name already exists, the copy is neither made nor overwritten. It shares the original's parent deformer, mesh, geometry keyforms, blend shapes, color, and visibility. Opacity is the original's opacity (the static value and the keyforms) multiplied by `seeThroughOpacity` (default 50%). The masks are the bangs among the candidates that still exist, are visible, and draw above the original at every keyform. If none remain, the copy is not made. Draw order sits half a generation interval above the highest order of those bangs (including keyforms), and below the lowest order of every other mesh. The copy is placed immediately before the original, in the same part, and hides with it when the part is hidden or fades out. A copy therefore follows every deformation, canvas edit, generated-result override, and bangs-order change of the original. Changing the setting only replays and does not trigger regeneration. Canvas drag preview updates the copies as well. Copies are not in the authored rig. An edit that names one returns `generated_copy`, and they cannot be picked on the canvas. **Eyes through bangs** uses another usual approach: opening a window in the bangs. Clip masks can only add, so a copy cannot be clipped by the eye white and the bangs at the same time. Copying the iris would draw iris outside the eye white onto the hair, and it would not disappear when the eye closes. So each bangs mesh that covers an unclipped eye layer (eye white, eyelashes, or an iris that is not clipped by eye white) is **inverted-clipped** in the finished rig by those eye layers, and a bangs copy clipped forward by the same mask is drawn above it (ID `<刘海 ID>_SeeThrough`). Its opacity is the bangs opacity multiplied by `1 − seeThroughOpacity`: hair inside the eye fades by the chosen ratio, the whole eye underneath draws as it did, and the iris and highlights clipped by the eye white show through on their own (`BuiltRig.seeThroughWindows` records the bangs and the eye layers that open the window). The copy's draw order is above the bangs and below any eyebrow copy that covers it, placed immediately before the bangs, in the same part. A layer that fades in and out with the eye opening and closing (the closed-eye line) cannot be a mask, because a mask ignores opacity. It is still copied above the bangs the way an eyebrow is. A layer clipped by its own mask (iris, highlights) shows through that mask's window and is not handled on its own. Bangs that already have their own mask or inverted mask are left without a window, reported as `bangs_clipped`. When the runtime target is below Cubism 4.0 (no inverted masks), no window is opened, reported as `target_unsupported`. Windows exist only in the finished rig. Authored bangs do not carry these masks. The clip IDs and the inverted mask that the inspector shows and edits are the document's own values (`authoredMasksOf`). In MCP `objects`, a bangs mesh's `topologyInfo.seeThroughWindow` lists the eye layers that open the window. Brows and eyes are both on by default: in an anime illustration both are often covered by bangs, and showing the brows and eyes through them is the usual approach. The settings are `seeThroughBrows`, `seeThroughEyes`, and `seeThroughOpacity` (CLI `--no-see-through` turns both off, `--no-see-through-eyes` turns off only the eyes, `--see-through-opacity`). A project saved before these keys existed reads them as off, and the rig is unchanged on reopen. A project saved by 3.6.5 carries `seeThroughEyes: false`, which was the default then, stays off, and looks the same. A project that had the eye lines on in 3.6.5 shows the whole eye through on reopen (the eye-line part looks the same; the eye white and the iris newly appear). An imported cmo3 / moc3 does not make copies. Meshes of the same name in it are kept as they are. Limits: Glue, deform paths, and vertex groups are not copied onto a copy (Glue would pull the partner mesh twice). A live simulation frame affects only the original. Baked keyforms are copied as usual. When the original is split or replaced, or every bangs mesh disappears, copies are no longer made, and `quality.overrides` reports `SEE_THROUGH_DROPPED` (info). A copy does not correspond to a source layer. MCP reports the original's layer.

These are preset algorithms, not a general 3D reconstruction. Layering in the original art, anchor error, wide hair pieces, extreme angles, and overlapping regions can all need manual reshaping. Exact curve constants and mesh divisions change with the implementation. When maintaining them, check the source. Do not copy a formula that has left its version behind.

## Default parameters

The table below comes from StandardParameters. In mesh mode, with deformers turned off, or when the corresponding parts are missing, the actual parameter set can differ. The user can also add parameters and variants.

| `ParamAngleY`, `ParamAngleZ` | -30…30 | 0 |
| `ParamBodyAngleX`, `ParamBodyAngleY`, `ParamBodyAngleZ`, `ParamBodyLean`, `ParamProportion` | -10…10 | 0 |
| `ParamEyeLOpen`, `ParamEyeROpen` | 0…1 (0…2 when `eye.wide`) | 1 |

| `ParamEyeLBounce`, `ParamEyeRBounce` (`eye.aftershock`, physics output) | -1…1 | 0 |
| `ParamBrowLY`, `ParamBrowRY` | -1…1 | 0 |
| `ParamBrowLAngle`, `ParamBrowRAngle` (`brow.shape`, −1 inner end lowered … +1 raised) | -1…1 | 0 |

The 1…2 segment of `eye.wide` keeps the eye shape from the original art. The upper lid lifts as a whole, the eye corners and the lower lid move with it, and the local arc is adjusted. 1 matches the original art exactly. The eye white and the eyelashes share the opening boundary. The closed-eye line stays hidden across the whole wide-open range. The default is only a gentle lift. The iris keeps the size and position from the original art. Pupil shrink is a separate emotion control. The closing segment is kept on its own.

## Tool comparison and gaps

The table compares each tool related to deformation and keyform editing with the published features of Cubism Editor (the Cubism column uses the generic names from its manual). Status: **Present** means it was already implemented; **This round** means it was filled in during this round; **Later** is recorded in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md). Mesh topology editing (adding and deleting points, connecting edges) and the canvas handles for interior modeling are tracked separately and are not in this table.

| Category | Feature in Cubism | PSD2Live | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selection | Click, marquee, lasso, brush select, select all / invert / connected | Select (V), Lasso (L), Brush select (W), `Ctrl+A` / `Ctrl+I` / `Shift+L`, hold Z for a temporary select; a mesh can be selected by point / edge / face | Present |
| Transform | Transform box: move, scale, rotate, anchor | Transform tool (T): drag the body to move, corners / edges to scale, the circle to rotate; the anchor can be dragged. Alt from the center, Shift for uniform scale or 15°, X / Y axis constraints. Precise Transform in Tool Details | Present |
| Soft selection | No direct equivalent (the deform brush is used instead) | Soft selection (O): while dragging or transforming selected points, surrounding points follow with distance falloff. Radius and falloff curve are adjustable. The canvas shows the influence with a red circle. The commit is an ordinary geometry edit | This round |
| Deform brush | Deform brush (push, smooth, inflate / deflate) | Push/pull (B), Smooth (Shift+B), Inflate / deflate (I); round / line / rectangle tips, radius, hardness, strength, 8 falloffs, connected items only | Present |
| Temporary deform tool | One-shot path / mesh / free deform | Temporary deform (lattice) (Shift+T): lay a one-shot 1×1–4×4 lattice on the selected points or the whole object. Dragging a lattice point moves those points by bilinear interpolation. Each release is one history step. Scale and rotate use the transform tool. Path-style bending uses a deform path | This round |

| Rotation deformer | Create, drag the origin, drag the handle angle, scale | Create rotation (R); drag the origin to move, drag an endpoint to turn (Shift for 15°), Alt + endpoint to scale | Present |
| Deform path | Deform path | Deform path (P / D). Edit mode changes the binding, deform mode writes the shape, and export bakes it into mesh keyforms | Present |
| Glue | Glue and weights | Glue (G): glue brush, weight brush, merge again by distance | Present |
| Keyform copy / paste | Copy and paste of a shape | Deform context menu “Keyform → Copy / Paste” (`Ctrl+Alt+C` / `Ctrl+Alt+V`). Copy freezes the shape and channels of the selected objects. When points are selected, only those points are pasted. MCP `keyform_apply op=paste` (`op=copy` copies the whole key and the channels) | This round |
| Shape blend, special paste | Blend shapes by ratio; paste only the checked properties; flip paste | “Paste with options…”: any of shape, opacity, draw order, multiply color, and screen color; a blend ratio; flip left/right or up/down (a Warp reverses columns / rows, a mesh maps vertices by the mirror of the rest position and must be symmetric, a rotation deformer's angle change is negated). | Paste targets for several shapes | Confirm and adjust the source-to-target correspondence before pasting | “Paste with options…” lists source → target row by row. A row can be changed to another suitable object or excluded. An ordinary paste pairs them automatically | This round |
| Keyform reset | Reset of a shape | “Reset to the default-key shape” (`Ctrl+Alt+X`), and for a mesh “Reset to the mesh rest shape”. Either can apply only to the selected points. MCP `op=reset` | This round |
| Keyform interpolation | Generate the in-between shape from the keys on both sides | “Interpolate from neighbouring keys of (parameter)” (`Ctrl+Alt+I`). Rebuilds the current key by interpolating between the keys on both sides according to position. MCP `op=interpolate` | This round |
| Create a key from the current shape | Add a key at the current value | The parameter panel adds a key on an empty spot. The new key takes the interpolated shape there. MCP `keyform_apply op=seed` samples the current interpolated shape at the specified key | Present |
| Flip left/right / mirror | Flip a shape left/right, copy symmetrically | “Mirror keyform” (`Shift+M`): to the symmetric key, to the opposite object, flip in place, all keys | Present |
| Symmetric editing | Live symmetry while editing | Mirror only after editing | Later |
| Multi-key editing | Change draw order, opacity, and color of several keyforms at once | “Multi-key editing…”: choose a range by parameter key values, and change opacity, draw order, multiply color, and screen color by overwrite / add / multiply / screen. MCP `keyform_apply op=multi_key`. Simultaneous multi-key editing of geometry is not available yet | This round |
| Automatic four-corner composition | Automatically generate the shapes of the four corners | Diagonal corners have to be made one by one | Later |
| Parameter interpolation | Extended interpolation and similar options | Interpolation between keys is linear | Later |
| Restore deformer shape | Restore the recorded original shape | “Restore every key to the default-key shape”: every key of the object returns to the default-key shape (the original shape is not recorded separately). MCP `op=reset all_keys=true` | This round |

| Simulation weights | No direct equivalent | Weight brush (Shift+V), weight gradient (Alt+V) | Present |

Keyform copy / paste, reset, interpolation, and multi-key editing expand into ordinary `set` operations at commit time, the same way mirroring does. Replay does not read other keys again. The GUI and MCP use the same `keyform_apply` command. A geometry `set` on a keyform owned by a generator is recorded as a generated-result override. A channel `set` is an ordinary channel keyform. Soft selection and the temporary deform lattice only decide where each point goes during one drag. What is committed is still that canvas geometry edit.

## Verification

Check the neutral pose, the endpoints, the combined corners, and the in-between values. Check whether parent motion and local shape apply the same motion twice. The export pipeline includes geometry diagnostics and a read-back check, but a warning is not proof that every pose has been verified. A feature the runtime target does not support can also be downgraded.
