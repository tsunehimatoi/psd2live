# Materials and layout

[Documentation](../README.md) · [User guide](USER_GUIDE.md) · [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md) · [Layout manifest](../spec/LAYOUT_MANIFEST.md)

Layout lets you place parts in the project first, then generate the rig. After generation you can keep adding images and changing tags, then generate again. Edits you have already made are kept.

## Start

- **Start from an empty canvas**: on the Start page, choose "Layout project" ("File > New > New layout project" also opens this). Pick a common size, or enter the width and height in the property panel, then click "Create layout project" or double-click a size. The new project has no rig yet, and it switches to the Layout workspace automatically.
- **Start from a PSD**: import the PSD as usual. The project generates a rig immediately, and every PSD layer also appears in the material panel, linked to the layer on the canvas.
- **Start from a layout manifest**: a [layout manifest](../spec/LAYOUT_MANIFEST.md) written by an external tool (for example an AI part splitter) — a JSON file, or a zip with that file at its root — is opened the same way as a PSD, or dropped straight into the window. Layers are placed from the manifest: position, stacking order, groups, tags, and variants are already set, and small parts keep more pixels than the canvas. The project has no rig yet, and it switches to the Layout workspace automatically.
- Any workspace can open the "Materials" panel from the Window menu.

The Layout workspace has the material panel on the left, the edit canvas (object mode) in the center, and the hierarchy tree and the layer table on the right.

## Material panel

- **Import**: click "Import" and choose files, click "Import folder" and choose a whole folder, or drop files and folders onto the panel (drop onto a folder, or onto a level of the path bar, to import there). A folder is imported with its original nesting. Each level of subfolder becomes a material folder. Files are ordered by the numbers in their names (arm2 comes before arm10). Hidden files and folders (such as .git) and unsupported files are skipped. Supported formats are PNG, BMP, TIFF, WebP (including lossy), JPEG, the image of each ArtMesh in a PSD or CMO3, and SVG. A PSD becomes a folder, a layer group becomes a subfolder, and each layer remembers its position in the PSD.
- **Browse**: on the left is a compact material tree (folders and the images inside them, expanded by default; the tree button on the toolbar collapses it; images in the tree can also be dragged onto the canvas or double-clicked to open). On the right is the contents of the current folder: folders first, then materials, and you can switch between grid and list. Drag the divider between the two columns to change the width. The grid thumbnail size is adjusted with the slider at the bottom right (a material with a lot of transparent margin shows only the part that has content). Double-click a folder to enter it. Go back with the tree, with ↑ on the path bar above, or by clicking any level. Search looks through every folder. The number at the top right of an item is the number of layers that use it.
- **Source**: a folder imported from a PSD or CMO3 is shown as a file icon with a "PSD" / "CMO3" ribbon, a page stacked behind it, and the item count and an arrow at the bottom right, meaning you can double-click to open it. Hover to see the source file name.
- **Organize**: drag a material or a folder onto another folder (the grid, the tree, or any level of the path bar) to move it in. The right-click menu pops up from the click, matching the canvas right-click menu. The menu of the tree's top level and of a folder can create a folder, or import files or a folder here. Right-click empty space in the grid or list to create or import in the current folder, and to switch among grid, list, and folder tree. Right-click empty space in the tree to create or import at the top of the library. The item menu can rename, edit tags, move up one level, and delete. Tags are separated by commas. Leave the field empty to clear them. Folder tags are searchable, and materials placed from the folder inherit them. A material that a layer or a reference image is using cannot be deleted, and a folder that contains such a material cannot be deleted. The menu shows the reference count. Delete uses a warning color. While the project is busy, modifying operations are unavailable.
- **Default reference layer**: right-click a material or a folder and choose "Place as a reference layer by default" to toggle the default type used when its materials are dragged onto the canvas. A folder applies to materials in every subdirectory. "Place on canvas" in the menu still places a model layer explicitly, and "Add as reference > In the layer stack" still places a reference layer explicitly.
- **Materials are the only source of layers**: every layer shows a material. Importing a PSD, a CMO3, or an image, splitting a layer, and drawing a mesh all register one automatically. A material that only you use follows that layer's edits. When several layers share one material, painting any of them paints the material, and the other layers update with it. To edit one of them alone, right-click "Make a separate material". For layers in an old project that do not have a material yet, the top of the panel prompts "Register in the material library".
- **SVG**: right-click "Texture resolution…" and enter a size such as `512x256`. The resolution decides the pixels that enter the atlas. The size on the canvas stays the same. Layers that are already placed update together.
- **Export PSD**: right-click a folder and choose "Export folder as PSD…".

## Material window

Double-click a material (or right-click "Open in the material window") to open a separate window. You can resize it freely and move it to another screen. While the window is open, click another material in the panel and the window switches to it.

- **View**: zoom with the wheel, and pan with the middle mouse button or by holding Space and dragging. The bottom right switches checkerboard / white / black. "Fit window" (F) and 1:1 (Home). At 2× and above it displays pixel by pixel.
- **Paint**: the window is the canvas paint mode. The toolbar on the left, the options bar at the bottom, the paint-session bar at the top (undo, redo, discard, apply), the right-click menu, the brush-tip and color-pick display, and every shortcut match paint mode. The only difference is that you paint on the material's own pixels. Color and the size of each tool are brought over from the current canvas, and brought back when the window closes.
- **Apply**: as in paint mode, strokes stay in the session first. "Apply" (Enter) writes them into the material as one history step. "Discard" throws them away. Switching to another material or closing the window applies automatically. During painting you can keep editing other content in the main window as usual.
- **Adjust**: the "Adjust" menu at the top left can flip horizontally or vertically, and adjust hue, saturation, value, and contrast. Dragging a slider previews first. Click "Apply" to write it. When there are unapplied strokes, apply or discard them first.

Every layer that has already placed this material updates together. Position and size on the canvas stay the same. After an SVG material is painted or adjusted, it becomes a bitmap at the current resolution.

## How materials and layers stay linked

A layer placed from a material (and a layer from an imported PSD) is linked to the material: the layer displays the material's pixels, and the material is the only source.

- Painting this kind of layer in paint mode paints the material itself. Every layer that uses this material changes together. The session bar says "Painting material “…” · N layers share it". You can paint anywhere on the canvas. On apply, the part that lands on the layer mesh is written into the material. The part outside the mesh is split into a new layer, placed directly above it, and stored as a new material beside the original material.
- In the layer table, a linked layer name is followed by a chain-link mark. Hover to see the material name and the number of layers that share it. Open it to "Open in the material window" or "Unlink".
- After you unlink, the layer's pixels belong to it. Further painting does not affect the material or the other layers. Other edits that make a layer's pixels differ from the material (for example a split, or replacing it with another image) also unlink automatically.
- "Replace the image of the selected layer" with another material changes the layer so it is linked to that material.

Materials are saved with the project. Every change can be undone.

## Placement

- **Drag a material or a whole folder onto the canvas** and it is placed at the drop point. You can also right-click "Place on canvas". Materials inside a folder keep their relative positions, and subfolders become source-image groups.
- **Dropping files and materials**: dropping a file from the computer and dragging a material from the material panel use the same drop targets. Where you release decides what happens, and the hint beside the pointer says so first. Drop onto the material panel to import as a material (drop onto a folder to import there). Drag an image onto the hierarchy tree: drop it on a deformer to put it under that deformer, or drop it on the upper or lower half of a mesh to insert it in front of or behind that mesh. When you drag onto the edit canvas, large options appear below the canvas: three kinds of reference (background / in the layer stack / foreground, each stating whether it enters the model), replace the image of the selected layer, and import into the material library only (files only). Release elsewhere on the canvas to place a layer there. A project, PSD, CMO3, or layout manifest dropped somewhere else opens. A position that does not accept the drop does not take it, and it does not report an error.
- Material that comes from a PSD is placed back at its position in the PSD by default: the PSD canvas is placed as a whole in the center of the project canvas, and scaled down uniformly when it is larger than the project canvas. When the two sizes match, that is the original position. Other materials are placed in the center of the canvas at "1 material pixel = 1 canvas unit", and shrink automatically when they extend past the canvas.
- Canvas units are the model's coordinates. After export to Cubism they are the pixels of the model canvas. They decide the model's size and proportions, and they do not decide sharpness. Sharpness comes from the material's own pixels. Placement and scaling do not resample. The smaller you place a layer, the denser its texture. On export the textures still have to fit the atlas budget (page size and page count in the texture workspace). When they do not fit, tiles shrink together.
- In object mode, use the transform box to **move, scale, and mirror** a layer. Pixels are not resampled: shrink a 600×600 eye into a 64×64 canvas region, and the atlas still stores it at 600×600 density (limited by the atlas budget).
- **Stacking order**: a newly placed layer is on top. Placing a whole folder keeps the folder's original stacking order. After you select a layer, use "Move up one" and "Move down one" on the layer-table toolbar, or press Ctrl+] / Ctrl+[ on the canvas to move up or down one layer, and Ctrl+Shift+] / Ctrl+Shift+[ to move to the front or the back. The layer table lists layers from back to front. Further down is further in front, matching Cubism draw order. Layers that have not been generated yet can all be reordered. After generation, the front-to-back order of generated layers is adjusted with draw order. A newly placed layer can still be reordered until the next generation. Until then it is shown temporarily at the front.
- **References**: comparison images such as the original art, a design sheet, or a sketch are a kind of member in the hierarchy tree (the reference icon), and there can be several. Each reference is shown in one of three places, and the display mode decides whether it enters the model: **Background** (does not enter the model; in the editor only, it sits under the whole model), **In the layer stack** (enters the model: it is a four-point rectangular ArtMesh in the model, added without pixel-outline tracing or full mesh generation, placed among the layers by draw order, and it can be parented under a deformer and follow the deformation; on export it belongs to the reference-image part and is written only when "Export guide layers" is checked in the export window), **Foreground** (does not enter the model; in the editor only, it covers the whole model). A reference in the layer stack enters the canvas as an editable rectangular ArtMesh, with no need to generate a rig first. To add one: drag an image or a material onto the edit canvas and choose the display mode from the large options below the canvas; or use the reference-image button on the hierarchy toolbar or on the display bar at the bottom right of the canvas (added in the foreground); or right-click a material and choose "Add as reference". Click a background or foreground reference in the hierarchy tree and handles appear on the edit canvas: drag inside to move, drag a corner for uniform scale, drag the circle above to rotate. A history entry is recorded only when you release. A reference in the layer stack is adjusted with the transform box, like any other ArtMesh. Right-click a reference: "Display mode" switches among the three places (each item states whether it enters the model; the material, position, display, and pins are all kept), plus show/hide, opacity and lock (background and foreground), restore as a model layer (in the layer stack), and remove. Alt+R toggles whether the current canvas shows background and foreground references.
- **Turn a layer into a reference**: a layer that has not taken part in generation yet (any layer before generation, and a layer placed later) and an imported image can become a reference in the layer stack with "Use as reference" on the layer-table toolbar or with Ctrl+Alt+R. The canvas right-click menu also has a "Use as reference" toggle. A reference layer has a reference icon after its name. Click it to restore a model layer. One that has already entered the model keeps its mesh as an ordinary layer. A layer marked `reference` in a layout manifest opens as a reference in the layer stack. A layer that has already been generated cannot be set as a reference.
- **Pin to a keyform**: references for different angles (front, side, looking up, and so on) can stack in the same place, each shown only on the matching keyform. One reference can also appear on several keyforms. All three display modes can do this. Right-click a reference. "Pin to keyform" at the bottom of the menu lists, in the shape of the parameter panel, the parameters in the model that have keyforms: a one-dimensional parameter is a slider with keyform dots, and a paired parameter is a 2D pad. Click a keyform dot to pin there (filled). Click again to clear it. The reference is shown on any dot it is pinned to (each parameter is within 0.5% of the parameter range of the dot) and hidden in the other poses. A gray mark is the current pose. "Unpin" clears every dot. The submenu of the same name on the canvas right-click menu is the same panel. Pinning only decides when the editor shows it. It is not written into the export.
- **Replace an image**: select the layer first, then right-click a material and choose "Replace the image of the selected layer". The layer's position and size on the canvas stay the same. Only the pixels and the atlas tile change. Right-click replace in the atlas view works the same way.

## Tags

Each layer in the layer table has a **Tags** cell. Click it to edit:

- Part and side words tell the generator what it is. For example "hair + front" is front hair, "eye + pupil + left" is the left-eye pupil, and "mouth + open" is an open mouth. Below the menu you can choose the part and the side directly.
- The input box accepts words from the vocabulary, and also common layer names (such as "front hair" or "eye-l"), which are converted to the matching words. Other text becomes your own tag, used only for organizing and search, and it does not affect generation.
- Part-role words say what the layer is inside the part: `highlight` (highlight), `pupil` (pupil), `lower` (lower eyelashes), `lid` (double-eyelid line), `shadow` (shadow), `blush` (blush), `side` (side hair), `ahoge` (ahoge), `ponytail` (ponytail), `braid` (braid), `animal` (animal ears), `ribbon` (ribbon), `pendant` (pendant, tassel), `rigid` (a hard ornament such as a button or a badge), `sleeve` (sleeve), `skirt` (skirt), and, for a layered mouth, `upperlip` and `lowerlip` (the upper lip and lower lip, including a ring of surrounding skin) and `upperline` and `lowerline` (upper lip line and lower lip line). During generation they are inferred from the layer name, the folder it is in, and the position among layers, and shown in the tags. When the inference is wrong, rewrite it. After you delete an automatically inferred role, it is not inferred again. Roles only affect modules under "Parts and modules". They do not change the existing generation method.
- Toggle variants and switch variants are not in the tags. They are set in the "Variant" column beside them.
- After you add tags to a material or a material folder, layers placed from it carry those tags automatically.

## Generate and regenerate

The generate bar at the top right of the edit canvas shows what is still waiting:

- **Not generated**: a newly created layout project. A layer has a real mesh as soon as it enters the canvas. You can edit meshes, deformers, parameters, and the hierarchy, and you can export. An empty canvas can be edited the same way. After you have placed things, click "Generate" to generate the automatic rig from the tags.
- **N waiting to generate**: layers placed after generation, and layers whose tags or classification changed. A newly placed layer immediately supports every edit and export of an ordinary workspace. It joins the automatic rig on the next generation. A changed classification does not affect the rig until the next generation (the layer table outlines it in the emphasis color).
- After you click "Regenerate", this content is merged into the rig. Keyforms, deformers, physics, swing, skeletons, and Glue you have made are kept. Content that cannot be migrated cleanly is listed in the quality check.
- A layer whose image was replaced regenerates its mesh from the new pixels by default. To keep the original mesh, choose "Generate but keep the geometry of replaced images" in the menu under the Generate button.

One generation takes about as long as importing a PSD of the same size.
