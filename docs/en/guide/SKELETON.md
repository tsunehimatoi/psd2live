# Bones and poses

[Documentation](../README.md) · [Quick reference](USER_GUIDE.md) · [Physics](PHYSICS.md)

Bones bake the body, the limbs, the tail, and the wings into a structure Cubism can run directly. The upper and lower body are **warp deformers** at the end of the body deformer chain. **Rotation deformers** turn whole parts such as the limbs. A mesh that crosses a joint finishes a smooth bend with **correction keyforms**. The exported moc3/cmo3 does not depend on this application, and it can be edited further in Cubism Editor.

## Workflow

Bone actions are gathered on the Bones tab. It shares a panel with the Hierarchy, as two tabs side by side (an old layout adds this tab on its own; it can also be opened or closed from the Window menu). The tree lists every bone by hierarchy. The drawables bound to a bone are listed under it, marked with the same color, and the names shown are the layer names (the names given when they were split). Clicking a drawable name selects the matching layer in the hierarchy. While the skeleton is being edited, it selects the bone that drawable belongs to.

**The skeleton is its own selection**, alongside drawables and deformers. Posing and editing the skeleton both happen in the canvas Skeleton mode (the mode menu, default `Alt+5`). Deform, Edit, and the other modes do not act on the skeleton:

| Mode | Skeleton |
| --- | --- |
| Object | Bones are drawn faintly on the canvas. Clicking a bone (or clicking a bone row on the Bones tab) selects the skeleton. Selecting a drawable or a deformer clears the skeleton selection. The Bones toggle in the view options can hide this layer of bones. Once hidden, bones cannot be clicked on the canvas, and they can still be selected from the tab |
| Skeleton | The toolbar has two tools, Pose Bones (default `Shift+W`) and Edit Bones (default `Alt+W`). Pressing either key in any mode enters Skeleton mode. On entry, an enabled skeleton uses Pose Bones, and otherwise Edit Bones. Selecting a drawable or a deformer in Skeleton mode returns to Object mode |

1. **Create / edit the skeleton**: in Skeleton mode choose the Edit Bones tool, or in Object mode right-click a drawable and choose Create skeleton / Edit skeleton. When the project has no skeleton yet, entering Skeleton mode does not generate one immediately. It shows a Create skeleton button in the center of the canvas. Clicking it (or the button of the same name on the Bones tab) creates it. The red trash can at the top right of the tab, Delete skeleton, removes the whole skeleton (one history entry, and it can be undone), and it can be created again afterward. While editing, each bound drawable is filled on the canvas, half transparent, in the color of the bone it belongs to. The drawables of the selected bone stand out more. Creation infers a skeleton automatically:
   - **Bind by semantics**: bind by layer tags and side. An arm binds the handwear on the same side, a leg binds legwear, a foot binds footwear, and a tail or wings bind their own layers. Toggle and switch variant layers are bound together, and they do not take part in estimating bone positions.
   - **Place by body structure**:
     - The shoulder joint sits below the torso’s shoulder line, inside the torso’s edge. The hip joint sits below the hip line (it is placed the same way when a skirt covers it).
     - A bent elbow or knee sits inside the outer corner of the bend. A straight limb is placed by proportion: upper arm : forearm : hand ≈ 1 : 0.85 : 0.55, and the thigh ≈ the shin.
     - The wrist is placed by the forearm-to-hand proportion. When there are shoes, the ankle sits at the shoe opening, and the toe takes the far end of the shoe.
     - A tail is split into 3–4 segments along the center axis inside the mesh, and they form one flexible bone chain (see Flexible bone chains below). When the center axis is fairly straight, the segments are equal. When there is a clear turn, the joint is placed at the turn, so each bone’s chord stays closest to the center axis.
   - Drag a joint end to adjust its position. Click an ArtMesh to bind it to the current bone.
   - In the Edit sub-tool, Shift-click adds or removes from the selection, dragging on empty space box-selects, and dragging a bone body translates the whole selection. The Tool details panel can select all, select a bone chain, rotate, and scale. Rotation and scale use the center of the selection’s ends as the axis, and including descendants can be turned on. An endpoint connected to the outside follows. A disconnected endpoint stays put.
   - The Tool details panel also provides duplicate, mirror, subdivide, and merge. Duplicate does not bind meshes by default, and the bindings can be transferred to the copy. Include descendants controls whether the whole bone chain is copied. Mirror can set the X axis, reverses side and direction automatically, and shows how many opposite-side meshes can be matched unambiguously. An ambiguous layer stays unbound. With symmetric editing on, a mirrored partner’s joint moves and selection transforms move together.
   - Subdivide splits the current limb bone into 2–16 segments, keeps the first segment’s ID and bindings, and hangs child bones on the last segment. Merge folds the currently connected child bone into a limb parent that has no branch, and migrates the bindings. The built-in body and head are not subdivided or merged. Their copies are custom bones. Subdivide and merge rebake from the rest skeleton, and they clear the mirror pairs that are affected.
   - **Sub-tools**: under Edit Bones on the left toolbar, Edit / New Bone / Extrude / Bind / Weights are listed (`1`–`5`; the canvas context menu can switch them too). Edit moves joints and binds meshes. New Bone drags from the start to the end and creates an independent custom bone with no parent. Extrude drags from the selected bone’s end, or clicks empty space, to create a child bone, and selects the new bone so the chain can keep extending. A preview is shown while dragging, and the bone is created on release. A length under 4 screen pixels is not created. A new bone binds no mesh by default. Bind it after switching back to Edit. Add bone at the top of the tree keeps the shortcut that extends 60 pixels downward by default, and selects the new bone.
   - A bound mesh computes weights automatically over the **whole limb**. A mesh of the same limb only needs to be bound to one of its bones.
   - **Flexible**: checking Flexible (smooth curve) in the bone panel makes the selected bone one segment of a flexible bone chain. A soft, long part such as a tail, a fin, or a finger uses it to bend into one smooth curve, instead of kinking at the joints. Several flexible children under one flexible bone are a branch point, for example the upper and lower lobes of a fish tail. A branch point can only sit on a joint. Subdivide first when a branch is needed in the middle of a bone.
   - **Straighten the bone chain**: select a bone on a flexible bone chain and click Straighten the bone chain. From the root, the chain turns segment by segment into one straight line. The drawn art unfolds along the chain with it (only mesh vertices move; the texture and UVs stay). The rest pose becomes straight, the art’s bend is saved as the pose “<bone name> drawn”, and each joint’s limits are widened automatically to include it. Keyforms and physics can then be keyed from the straight state. A branch stays open, and each branch is straightened on its own. Restore the drawn bend puts the bones and the mesh back to the art.
   - Click a bone in the tree, then click an ArtMesh on the canvas, to bind it. The × at the end of a drawable row unbinds it. A tail or wings can be added or removed with the checkboxes at the top of the tab.
   - Batch actions switch to the Bind sub-tool. Choose the target bone in the tree, then click or box-select meshes, or check them in the list in Tool details. Shift box-select adds meshes. The canvas highlights the meshes about to be acted on. Tool details shows the count and the target bone. Click Bind selected or Unbind selected to apply them together.
   - The Weights sub-tool selects a bound limb mesh, and a bone of the same limb in the tree. Click or drag to add, subtract, replace, or smooth weights. Blue to orange is the selected bone’s influence. The paint mode (add / subtract / replace / smooth), radius, strength, and the replace value are set on the tool options bar at the bottom left of the canvas (and in the context menu). The current angular skinning keeps one bone, or two adjacent bones, per vertex. Painting normalizes automatically.
   - Weight cleanup can choose 1–2 influences and a low-weight threshold, and it detects and repairs empty values, invalid references, and rows that are not normalized. Automatic weights can also be restored. Copy / mirror weights first chooses the source, the target, interpolation / nearest vertex / same topology, and the bone correspondence, then shows the matched and unmatched counts before applying. Unmatched vertices keep automatic weights. Nearest vertex has a distance limit, and mirror can set an axis. Same topology requires the vertex count and the triangle indices to match.
   - Hand weights are saved with the project, and they are used for preview and for the export bake. Weights are stored by rest position and triangle samples. Vertices added when a mesh is refined inherit them by interpolation. When a bone is subdivided, a hand-weighted mesh influenced by that bone returns to automatic weights, so the new bone segments are assigned again.
   - With a bone selected, the bottom of the tab can adjust Joint band width (how wide a range on both sides of the joint blends smoothly; Auto restores the default) and the Minimum / maximum angles. The angle range is that bone parameter’s range.
   - A name in the bone settings is saved on Enter or when the field loses focus. The bone ID and the parameter stay the same. The parent dropdown can change or clear the parent bone, and coordinates are kept by default. Turning on Connect to the parent bone’s end translates the whole subtree to the parent bone’s end. Turning it off keeps the parent and the position, and coincident ends no longer move together. The bone itself and its descendants cannot be set as the parent.
   - After the skeleton edit is done, Bone parameter sampling can adjust the tolerance (pixels), the minimum angle spacing, and the keyform cap per mesh. The model rebuilds while the values slide, and it shows the total number of parameter points actually generated. Each bone in the tree shows its parameter’s point count beside it. While it computes, Calculating parameter points is shown. The settings are saved with the project. Reset restores the defaults.
   - **Leaving the edit saves it**: switching to the Pose Bones tool, switching to another mode, selecting another layer, or clicking Done writes the changes as one history entry and rebuilds the model. After Done, an enabled skeleton switches to the Pose Bones tool to pose, and a disabled one returns to the mode from before Skeleton mode. Only Cancel discards this edit. Done and Cancel are on the mode bar, at the bottom of the Bones tab, and in the canvas context menu.
   - Disable at the bottom of the tab while editing only turns the skeleton off. The bones and the bindings are both kept. Click Enable on the tab to bring it back.
2. **Pose**: in Skeleton mode choose the Pose Bones tool. When the skeleton is enabled, entering Skeleton mode uses this tool by default.
   - Drag a bone body: FK. Only that bone rotates.
   - The left toolbar (under the pose tool) or the canvas context menu can choose Auto / FK / IK (`1`–`3`). Auto keeps a body drag as FK, and a tip drag or a Shift drag as IK. Explicit FK always turns only the current bone. Explicit IK always solves the bone chain.
   - With a bone selected, the IK settings in Tool details decide that end’s chain length (1–32 bones), iteration count, pixel tolerance, and bend direction. Each joint still obeys its own angle limits. After Pin selected tip, changing a parent parameter keeps solving. Dragging the cross on the canvas, or typing target coordinates, can change the position. Enable, stop, and release are supported. A target that cannot be reached, or that conflicts with another, shows the remaining error.
   - IK targets and settings are saved in the project. The exported model keeps the parameter ranges. Animation keeps the solved angles. Export does not include a runtime pinned target or an IK constraint. Save / insert a named pose can write the solved result into the animation.
   - Tool details of the Pose Bones tool can save the current model parameters by name (the same name overwrites), and apply or delete a stored pose after it is chosen. Apply skips parameters that have been removed, and clamps to the current parameter range. Open the animation editor, choose a snapshot under Insert stored pose, and it inserts every parameter that still exists at the playhead and creates the missing curves. Interpolation and handles of keyframes that already exist are kept.
   - Drag a bone’s end, or hold `Shift` and drag: IK. That bone, together with up to two parent bones, turns toward the pointer. Every joint obeys its angle limits.
   - The default angle ranges are wide (for example the upper arm −90°~150°, the forearm ±150°), so a drag is not stopped halfway. To limit a joint, reduce its minimum and maximum angles while editing the skeleton.
   - Posing changes parameter values only. It does not change meshes, and it does not create a history entry. Reset pose on the mode bar and in the canvas context menu zeros every bone parameter and pose parameter. Show skin weights in the display bar at the bottom right colors each vertex by bone color. The color inside a joint band is a mix of the two bones’ colors.
3. **Motions**: the motion panel shows the motions the current skeleton can play. The Motions group of Model presets has only two switches, Basic motions (idle, blink, nod, shake) and Skeleton motions. Turning one off removes the whole group from the motion panel, the animation editor, the preview, and export. Each motion’s switch and advanced parameters are kept, and turning it back on restores them. A single motion is enabled row by row in the motion panel. On export, if motions are checked, the matching `motion3.json` is written as well, and registered in the model3 motion group of the same name. Each preset is made of the **poses** in the table below plus the standard head and body parameters. Poses are of two kinds. **Pose parameters** in the model (crouch, weight, knees in, hop, tail swing, wing flap) have their effect already baked into the model, and a motion only decides when and how far to do it. **Gestures** (arm swing, arms in and out, raise a hand, wave, one foot kicked back) have no parameter in the model. When a motion is written they are converted into curves of bone-angle parameters, and added to the values the motion itself writes on the same bone parameters. The upper-arm rotation that comes with the leg-pose band is a gesture too. Leg poses (crouch, weight, knees in) only add. One motion uses only one of them, and the others stay at 0. A hop does not need a landing, and it can be stacked on top of them. A pose the skeleton cannot play is left out.

   Preset motions are not templates of fixed angles. At generation time the rest skeleton of the art is read first, for each arm’s direction and length, the head-to-body ratio, and the face position. The pose is then solved inside a comfortable human range (the shoulder stays within about 115°, the elbow does not reverse, the wrist stays within 35°). For example, a wave stops the hand beside the face at about eye height, and it does not cover the face. A shy pose brings both hands together in front of the body. In time there is an anticipation, an ease in and ease out, a lag that steps along the bone chain, and an overshoot that settles. The rest of the body (weight, body tilt, head) follows along. A chibi proportion is springier. Keyframes sit only on the beats, and Bezier curves carry the transition.

| Motion | Condition | Poses it drives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle (loops for 12 seconds) | Always | Relaxed, like a person standing: three breaths of different depth and length, a fast inhale and a slow exhale, the shoulders lift and the chest opens, the body nods a little later and the head later still. Weight is the sum of a 6-second component and a 4-second component, and it does not repeat inside the loop. The hips lead the weight change. The upper body and the body turn follow with a lag, in that order. The head tilts slightly the other way to keep the gaze level, turns last, and drifts slowly on its own. The arms hang naturally. The upper arm, forearm, and hand swing 2~4° with a lag that steps along the chain, and the two sides are not fully in sync. The tail swings slowly. The wings follow the breath. With no skeleton, only the body and head parts are kept. Curves place keyframes only at the turns |
| TailSwing (3 seconds) | A tail with a bound mesh | A tail whip, three trips by default, 1 second each |
| Crouch (2.2 seconds) | Leg bones | A small lift first, then a crouch. The body leans forward, the head lifts and looks ahead, and the arms come in front of the body a little later. The bottom springs back slightly. On the way up it leans back a little, then settles. Weight stays at 0 |
| WeightShift (3 seconds) | Leg bones | A light push the other way first, then the weight moves to one side, stands there, and comes back. The body and the head follow with a lag, in that order. The arms swing a beat later with the inertia. Crouch stays at 0 |
| Shy (3 seconds) | Leg bones, or arms with a bound mesh | Shy and fidgeting: a glance up first, then the head lowers and tilts and the gaze moves away. The knees turn in. Both hands come together in front of the body and rub a little. The body twists a little left and right |
| Wave (2.8 seconds) | Arms with a bound mesh (the right arm first) | A wave: the elbow bends and the hand rises along an arc to the side of the face, swings three and a half times about the elbow, weaker each time, and the wrist is a beat late. The weight moves to the other side, the body tilts slightly the other way, and the head leans toward the hand |
| HeadTilt (2.2 seconds) | Leg bones, or arms with a bound mesh | A head tilt: a small move the other way first, then a large tilt, a slight overshoot, and a beat of holding still. The body and the weight tilt the same way a little later. Both hands draw in slightly |
| Cheer (2.2 seconds) | Leg bones, or arms with a bound mesh | A happy cheer: a crouch and then a jump, speed on the takeoff and the landing, a bent-knee cushion on landing, and a smaller second jump. Both arms throw up beside the head on the first jump, rise and fall with the landing, and the elbows bend first as they come down. The head lifts. The tail and the wings flap quickly |
| LegKick (2.6 seconds) | Legs with a bound mesh | One foot kicked up: the weight moves onto the left foot, the right foot kicks back and wobbles lightly, both arms open to keep balance, and the head tilts |
| Sway (3 seconds) | Leg bones, or arms with a bound mesh | A happy sway: a light push, then two sways left and right that die down. Each part follows with a lag, in order. The arms swing with the inertia |
| IdleCute (loops for 12 seconds) | Leg bones | A cute idle: on top of Idle, knees-in stays at 0.3 and the hands-together-in-front pose is taken at 30%. The arms still swing loosely on that pose, and the weight no longer loops. It is exported to the Idle motion group, as a second idle besides the ordinary Idle. The preview plays one round |

Each preset in the motion panel expands to its advanced parameters. After a change, the preview, the animation editor, and export all take effect together. The values are saved with the project and can be undone:

| Motion | Advanced parameters |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | Swing (weight, body, arms, tail), breath (breath, body rise, wing flap), head (the three head angles), speed, blink switch |
| IdleCute | The same as Idle, plus how far the limbs draw in (the mix of knees-in and hands together, 0.3 by default) |
| Blink | How far it closes, speed, blink twice in a row |
| Nod / Shake | Amount, speed, count (each time a little weaker than the one before) |
| TailSwing | Amount, speed, count |
| Crouch / Cheer | Crouch depth or force, speed |
| The other skeleton presets | Amount, speed |

Amount scales a one-shot motion about its starting rest value, and a looping motion about the mean of the curve. Eye open and close does not change with amount. Speed scales keyframe times and the duration together. After a preset’s keyframes are edited in the animation editor, that preset becomes a manual override, the advanced parameters stop taking effect for now, and Restore default clears the override and the parameters together. Delete in the context menu removes the preset from the list and from export. It can be found again under Restore deleted presets in the New motion menu. The play button on the panel shares one playback state with the animation editor: a click opens that motion in the editor and plays it, and clicking again, or pausing in the editor, stays in sync.

When physics is exported, tail joints from the 2nd segment on, and the wings, are driven by physics. If every bone a pose involves is driven by physics (for example a wing flap), Idle no longer writes that pose, so the two do not conflict. Physics writes bone parameters. A tail whip and a wing flap are blend-shape deformations stacked on top, and both can be in effect at once.

## Tool overview

Skeleton mode has two main tools, Pose Bones and Edit Bones. Edit Bones has 5 sub-tools: Edit, New Bone, Extrude, Bind, and Weights. Pose Bones has Auto / FK / IK. Sub-tools are listed under the main tool on the tool bar. Switching keeps the same draft. Finish, or leaving the edit, saves it as one history entry. Cancel discards the draft.

| Area | What is already there | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edit | Single select, Shift add/remove, box select, bone-chain select, move / rotate / scale, drag a joint | Endpoints connected to the outside stay in sync. A transform can include descendants |
| New Bone | Drag to create an independent custom bone, with a preview, and it is selected automatically | No parent is set. Mesh bindings are not inherited automatically |
| Extrude | Extend a child bone from the selected bone’s end. Extrude can continue | Each create makes one custom bone |
| Tree actions | Add at the default length, delete (child bones hang on the original parent), tail and wing switches, rename, set / clear the parent, connect / disconnect a joint, duplicate, mirror, subdivide, merge | Parent candidates exclude the bone itself and its descendants. Connect translates the subtree. Disconnect keeps the position. Body bones cannot be deleted. Subdivide and merge are only for limb bones |
| Bind | Bind one mesh, and batch bind and unbind by click / box select / list checkbox, with a highlight preview | Each mesh names one binding bone. Weights inside a limb are computed automatically |

| Pose | Auto / FK / IK, IK pinned targets and solver settings, reset pose, save / apply a named pose, insert a snapshot in the animation editor | Apply and insert skip parameters that no longer exist, and clamp to the current range. ## Generated structure

| Object | ID | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Torso bend | `DeformSkelTorso` (warp deformer) | Hung under the breath deformer, and it takes over every child the breath deformer used to have (torso, clothes, arms, wings, tail, head rotation). The axes are the upper-body and lower-body parameters: above the waist follows the upper body, below the waist follows the lower body, both rotating about the waist, and the waist transition band bends smoothly. The legs are not in it. When the lower body turns, the skirt and the tail turn with it, and the feet stay on the ground |
| Bone rotation deformer | `DeformSkel_<bone ID>` | Generated only for the limbs, the tail, and the wings. The pivot is at the bone head (the joint at the parent bone’s end). The handle runs along the bone (a Cubism handle points up at 0° and clockwise is positive, so a bone that points straight down has `baseAngle` 180°, and a nested deformer records only the turn relative to the parent bone). They nest by the bone hierarchy. Arms, wings, and the tail hang under the torso bend. Legs hang under the leg warp (`DeformLegs`). Arms and wings parented directly to the body deformer also take body Y as an axis, and scale in perspective as the upper body leans forward and back (a warp’s scale does not reach a rotation deformer). A bone with no mesh or child deformer under it generates no deformer. A joint inside one mesh (it has no mesh of its own, and only connects to the next segment) is not generated on its own, and it is merged into the next segment’s deformer |
| Glue | `GlueSkel__<mesh A>__<mesh B>` | When one limb is split into several meshes, vertices that coincide on the seam are welded automatically |
| Bone parameters | `ParamArmLA`, `ParamArmLB`, `ParamHandL`, `ParamLegLA`, `ParamLegLB`, `ParamFootL`, `ParamTail1..n`, `ParamWingL`, and so on | The unit is degrees. A positive value swings away from the body, and the two sides agree. Limb bone parameters that can be added are blend-shape parameters. The rest are ordinary parameters (see Blend-shape bones) |
| Crouch | `ParamSkelCrouch` (0..1, blend shape) | The body sinks by 10% of the leg length, the knees bend toward the viewer and slightly outward, and the feet stay where they were |
| Weight shift | `ParamSkelWeight` (−1..1, blend shape) | The hips shift sideways and tilt slightly, and sink at the same time when that is needed, so both feet can still reach the ground. The upper body corrects back the other way. In a motion the upper arms follow afterward (a gesture) |
| Tail whip | `ParamSkelTailSwing` (−1..1, blend shape) | The tail curls toward one side, segment by segment, and it turns more toward the tip |
| Wing flap | `ParamSkelWingFlap` (−1..1, blend shape) | The left and right wings flap symmetrically |
| Knees in | `ParamSkelKneesIn` (0..1, blend shape) | The body sinks slightly, the knees turn inward, and the feet stay where they were. In a motion the upper arms draw in a little (a gesture) |
| Hop | `ParamSkelHop` (0..1, blend shape) | The body and the leg warp move up as a whole, and the legs leave the ground with them. Stacked on a crouch, it is a jump with bent knees |

Leg poses act on the body warp (the hip motion) and the leg warp (the legs bend by two-bone IK, and the feet do not move), so they still work when the legs are drawn on one layer and the leg bones have no bound mesh. When the legs are split and bound to the leg bones, the leg bones’ rotation deformers also turn by IK.

The gestures below have no parameter in the model. They are played by bone-angle parameters. A preset motion’s arm poses are solved from the body proportions. Of those, only LegKick’s one-foot kick still uses the gesture here:

| Gesture | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Arm swing | The left and right arms swing in opposite directions. The farther joint has a smaller amount |
| Arms in and out | −1 brings both hands together in front of the body (the upper arm, forearm, and hand all turn toward the inside of the body). +1 opens both arms and lifts them |
| Raise a hand (right) | Turns only the character’s right arm: the elbow rises to shoulder height, the forearm stands up, and it sits beside the face |
| Wave | The right forearm and the right hand swing left and right, stacked on the raised hand |
| One foot kicked back | +1 is the character’s left leg, −1 the right leg: the thigh draws in slightly, the shin flips outward, and the toe kicks up with it. It can be stacked on the weight shift |

## Algorithm

- **Deformer hierarchy**: when a rotation deformer hangs under a warp deformer, it inherits only the pivot position and the angle. A non-rigid deformation of the bend above does not pass down. When a warp deformer hangs under a warp deformer, only the child deformer’s control points are transformed. So the body bones do not use rotation deformers. They are made as a fine-mesh warp deformer at the end of the body chain: the bends of body X/Y, body Z, and breath travel all the way to the torso mesh, and the shoulder and head pivots move with them. The fine waist bend acts directly on the mesh and the limbs, and a coarse-mesh deformer above does not resample it. A warp deformer is an identity at rest. Inserting one moves nothing, and it does not change a child’s coordinates. After the skeleton is enabled, the shoulder line and waist line of breath and body Z are taken from the upper-body bone, so breath lifts only above the waist, and body Z tilts about the waist of the upper-body bone. The hip, knee, and ankle of the leg warp are taken from the leg bones. The body deformer structure is in [Deformer and parameter specification](../spec/DEFORMER_AND_PARAMETER_SPEC.md).
  ```
  DeformBodyXY（身体 X/Y：躯干转向，身体随髋部平移、下沉、站高；腿部姿势的髋部运动）
   └ DeformBodyLean（身体前后倾）
       └ DeformBodyZBreath（身体 Z、呼吸）
           └ DeformSkelTorso（上半身 + 下半身）
               ├ 躯干、衣服、裙子等网格
               ├ 手臂、翅膀、尾巴的旋转变形器
               └ DeformHeadRotation → 头部
  DeformLegs（身体 X/Y 和腿部姿势下的腿，脚掌不动）
   ├ 未绑定的腿部网格
   └ 腿骨的旋转变形器
       └ DeformSkel_<骨骼 ID>Stance（身体 X/Y 下的站姿）→ 绑定到这根骨骼的腿部网格
  ```
- **Waist bend**: the upper and lower body both use the waist (the bone head) as the axis. Distance to the waist along the bone blends with smoothstep inside the joint band width, and outside the band the turn is fully rigid. The default half-width is 25% of the bone length, capped at 45%, and the upper or lower body can be selected while editing the skeleton to adjust it.
- **Rigid parts**: a rotation deformer interpolates the angle, not vertex positions, so a limb is an exact rigid rotation at any parameter value, and it does not shorten between keyframes. When several joints turn at once, the keyforms do not have to be multiplied together.
- **Weights**: each vertex follows at most the two bones on either side of a joint. The bone closest to the vertex is its primary bone. Inside the joint band at either end of the primary bone, the blend uses smoothstep on the signed distance to the joint’s angle bisector. A joint band’s default half-width is 35% of the shorter of the two bones, capped at 45%, so the joint bands at the two ends of one bone do not overlap.
- **Angle blend**: a vertex inside a joint band rotates about the joint by weight × relative angle, instead of a linear blend between two rigid positions (LBS). The distance from the vertex to the joint stays constant, the limb width stays constant while bending, and the inside of the elbow does not collapse.
- **Where a mesh hangs**:
  - Each mesh prefers to hang under the rotation deformer of **the joint closest to the body that the mesh covers**. The join with the previous segment is then carried by the deformer directly, and a far bone’s turn does not make that join depend on keyform interpolation to cancel it. Only when the mesh covers no joint is the bone chosen by weights and by how strongly the parameters are coupled. The part of the mesh that reaches onto other bones is written into the mesh’s own keyforms by those bones’ angles.
  - **Unsplit** (one mesh covers the whole limb): if the mesh contains the shoulder, the elbow, and the wrist, it hangs under the upper arm, and the shoulder join follows the upper arm exactly. The elbow and the wrist bend in the mesh keyforms. If stockings are drawn from the middle of the thigh to the ankle, cover no hip joint, and do cover the knee, they hang under the shin. The thigh deformer above still carries the whole leg as the hip pivot. A bone with no mesh below it (for example the foot) generates no deformer.
  - **Split** (one mesh per segment): each segment hangs under its own bone’s rotation deformer. Vertices inside the joint band are still skinned by position. The two segments deform identically where they overlap, so the seam does not split. Vertices that coincide on the seam are also glued automatically.
  - **A joint in the middle** (for example the thigh and the shin are one mesh, hung under the thigh, and the foot is another): each mesh has at most one rotation deformer, and the shin is not generated on its own. The foot’s rotation deformer hangs directly under the thigh, and it takes the shin parameter as its own keyform axis. The angles add directly. The pivot takes keys along the knee’s arc, and the key spacing is taken from the distance to the knee, so the pivot stays within 1 px of the arc, which matches the mesh’s precision. When the shin is a blend-shape parameter, this part is written as a blend shape too.
- **Correction keyforms**: a keyform takes only the parameters of the joints it involves as axes. Each axis is divided evenly on both sides of 0, using the fewest keys that keep linear interpolation between two keys within the configured tolerance of the true arc. The default tolerance is 1 px, and the minimum angle spacing is 7.5°. A part close to the joint (for example only the joint band is affected) has sparse keys. A long rigid part outside the bone the mesh hangs on is what needs dense keys. One mesh’s keyform total is capped at 600 by default. Past that, keys are removed starting from the axis with the most keys. Every keyform is computed from the deformer transform the runtime actually builds, so a rigid part lands exactly where the deformer does. When it has to be fully exact, split the limb into segments.
- **Blend-shape bones**: a keyform mesh stores one shape for every combination of axes. A mesh that crosses the elbow and the wrist stores “forearm key count × hand key count” keyforms. If no vertex is influenced by both bones at once, the two joints’ bends only add, and a blend shape needs only “forearm key count + hand key count” shapes. When the export target is Cubism 5.0 or newer, a bone parameter that meets this is a blend-shape parameter: the rotation deformer keeps a single rest shape, the blend shape interpolates the angle linearly among the minimum, 0, and maximum keys, and the result is still an exact rigid rotation. The mesh’s correction on that bone becomes a blend shape too. A parameter type is only one kind for the whole model, so as soon as one place must multiply (a vertex is influenced by both bones at once, or two middle joints in a row fold into the same rotation deformer), that parameter stays an ordinary parameter. The tail and the wings are read and written by physics, and they are always ordinary parameters. The bone a mesh hangs on is decided first by which joints it covers, so the join stays stable. Only when the mesh covers no joint is a bone preferred that lets the joints stay independent of each other.

- **Joint shape templates**: the outer shape of a knee or an elbow is taken from the pose reference of the MHR v1.0.1 human model (`src/main/resources/skinning/mhr-joints.tsv`, extracted by `tools/research/extract_mhr_joint_templates.py`; the license is `licenses/MHR.txt`): 25 axial sections and 11 angles per joint, normalized by the half-width of the straight joint. `SkeletonJointTemplates` fits the width from where the mesh boundary meets the joint cross-section, and gets a local residual relative to linear skinning. It acts only within about three half-widths on either side of the joint, and it falls off smoothly. Hand weights are not recomputed. The inside of every connected joint that can fit a cross-section (including the wrist, the ankle, and a custom bone chain) folds onto the bend’s angle bisector by the same rule. The inside blend band may overlap and flip. The outside, and faces that do not take part in the fold, still have their triangle direction checked. The reference is a 2D projection of one neutral body on the bend plane. The outside of a bone chain whose rest angle exceeds 25°, whose cross-section does not close, or that is too wide, keeps the base ARAP. A new bone’s joint band defaults to 16 axial intervals, and it can be adjusted as Joint mesh density on the bone sampling panel (bone JSON version 10 stores `sampling.jointMeshSegments`; an old file defaults to 4).
- **Joint refinement**: a bound mesh inserts “joint mesh density” + 1 rows of vertices along the angle bisector inside each joint band (16 intervals and 17 rows by default; an old skeleton that lacks the setting has 4 intervals and 5 rows). A vertex is always inserted where a row meets the outer outline, so the outline can bend into an arc. A new vertex is an affine combination of the original vertices, so the picture does not move, and existing keyforms and Glue are remapped automatically. Baking again does not add more vertices.
- **Flexible bone chains**: the bones are the control polygon of the center line, like the handles of a path deformer. The center line starts at the root bone’s head, walks along the bones, turns a rounded corner at each joint, and ends at the tail of the last bone. A rounded corner takes the same distance before and after the joint (half of the shorter bone on the two sides) and connects them with a rational quadratic Bezier (the middle control point is the joint, and the weight is cos(turn / 2)). When the two sides are equal in length it is exactly an arc. The turn is spread evenly through the corner, instead of gathering on the joint. So however the bones are split, the bend stays smooth: short bones have a tight small corner between them, the middle of a long bone is straight, the whole curve’s tangent is continuous, and it does not bend back on itself. The tip is always at the bone’s tip, and the mesh is not shorter than the bones. At a branch, the stem walks straight to the branch point, and each lobe starts turning from the branch point. Turning one lobe does not affect the stem or the other lobe. Near the branch point, a lobe’s mesh blends from “rotating rigidly with the stem’s end” to “following the lobe’s curve”. The blend distance is half a lobe segment, and a concave corner does not tear.
  - **Bind by connectivity only**: samples are taken along each bone (only samples that land inside the drawn mesh; the part of a bone that sticks out of the art does not take part). The vertex closest to a sample is a seed. A multi-source shortest path then runs along the mesh edges, and each vertex belongs to the bone of the seed with the smallest mesh distance. Two stretches of tail that touch after curling, but are not connected on the mesh, do not pull each other. Arc length takes the vertex’s nearest point on the drawn curve, and it looks only within the bone it belongs to and half a segment before and after. The vertex records the arc length and the sideways offset from the curve, and afterward it rotates as a whole with the curve’s local coordinate system, independent of how far it is from the center line. Vertices of a flexible bone chain are decided entirely by the curve. They no longer go through the joint template and ARAP. A rigid parent bone (for example the palm) and the flexible root bone still blend by an ordinary joint band.
  - **Arc length**: arc length is computed from the bones’ rest length. Each stretch of curve corresponds to the bone length it represents and is mapped in proportion, so however a bone bends, a vertex stays at its relative position on that bone.
  - **Bake**: the root of the bone chain is still an exact rotation deformer. When a mesh is influenced by only one or two joints, the bend is written into the mesh’s correction keyforms, and the key count is fitted to the same tolerance as a rigid bone chain. When the whole mesh is on the flexible bone chain and is influenced by three or more joints (a long tail, a fish tail with a branch), it becomes one nested warp deformer per joint (`DeformSkel_<bone ID>Flex`). A keyform mesh would have to store the product of every joint’s key count. Six joints under the cap of 600 would leave each joint only the three keys −90°, 0°, and 90°, and halfway between them the mesh would interpolate linearly between the two keys’ shapes and shorten visibly. Each nested warp deformer takes only its own parameter as an axis, the key counts add, and they are taken fully at a 1 px tolerance. Each deformer rotates the part after the rounded corner about the joint. Each point inside the corner, by its fraction of the corner’s arc length, moves rigidly with the point and the tangent direction at the same fraction of the curve, so the corner gathers toward the bend, and the part in front of the joint does not whip the other way (rotating about the joint by weight alone swings the part in front of the joint the opposite way, and several segments bending together become a wave). That is what the curve looks like on the bones it has bent past, so wherever the deformer turns, the mesh lands on the bone’s curve at that moment, and the tip agrees with the bone. At a branch, a lobe rotates about the branch point by weight, from the branch point to the lobe’s midpoint. A mesh point’s arc length is taken from the nearest mesh vertex (from the connectivity-only binding). The mesh spacing is refined by the narrowest gap between neighbouring parts that are not connected (at most 64×64). An inner deformer can turn the mesh into an empty region of the outer mesh, and the empty region takes the weight the turned part should have. Parameters, physics, and poses such as the tail whip stay the same. The tail whip is added on each deformer as a blend shape. A flexible bone’s parameter is not made a blend shape.
  - **Straighten**: before straightening, the stem of the bone chain (the root bone to the branch point or the tip) is fitted to the drawn center line. Every 6 px along the chain, the middle of the drawn outline is taken (an end cut whose section is only six tenths of the front-to-back width does not count). The joints are moved by least squares so the center-line curve passes through those points. The root bone’s head does not move. Each joint has a weak pull back toward where it was. Lobes after a branch translate with the branch point. When the center line does not follow the art, each place it misses keeps that distance on either side of the straight line, and it becomes a wave. When a joint is drawn on the art’s center line, the curve also cuts into the inside of the bend at the turn. After the fit, it is straightened. Each bone records `drawnBend`, the angle by which the art bends more at that bone’s head than the bone’s rest geometry. Binding is done on the drawn geometry (the rest skeleton bent back by `drawnBend`), and the rest mesh is placed on the rest curve. Each joint takes `drawnBend` as an extra key, so the drawn pose is reproduced exactly on the keyforms. Bone JSON version 11 stores `flex` and `drawnBend`. When a skeleton from before version 11 is read, a tail bone chain becomes a flexible bone chain automatically. A mesh that goes through nested warp deformers is placed on the rest curve the same way. When the deformer turns to the art’s bend, the mesh lands back on the drawn curve, so the drawn pose is reproduced within the mesh’s precision.
  - **A mesh hung somewhere else**: a mesh that history has hung on a non-bone deformer (or wrapped in its own warp) does not go through the bake. It is skinned after history replay, and it bends along the flexible bone chain’s curve the same way. Straightening cannot move that mesh’s rest vertices in this step, so the displacement from “unfold the art to the straight line” is written into each of its keyforms (including the rest key). A blend shape is measured from the rest key.
- **Pose parameters and gestures**: a mesh that crosses a joint writes the part on the other side of the joint into its own shape, and where that part lands depends on the joint’s **total angle**. A blend shape can only add the displacements each parameter causes on its own, and the sum of two partial turns of the same joint is not the displacement of turning through the total angle once. When a pose is stacked on a bone angle, or two poses turn the same joint, the limb leaves the bone, and at a large angle the error can reach a hundred pixels or more. So a limb joint is turned only by its own bone parameter. A pose that only turns limb joints is a gesture, and a motion converts it into bone angles to play it. Each track of a motion is a cubic curve in time between keys (the Bezier handles are fixed at 1/3 of the time). A pose is linear between neighbouring keys, so the bone curve is a cubic between those moments too. The conversion samples each key, and each moment a pose passes through one of its own key values, and it carries the slopes on both sides, so the bone curve agrees with the pose exactly. The model keeps only the poses a bone parameter cannot replace: the hip motion of a leg pose (IK keeps both feet on the ground) and the upper body correcting back, and the tail and wings driven by physics. They are stacked on the related objects as blend shapes: the body and leg warps (hip motion and the leg bend), the torso bend (the upper body correcting back the other way), the bone rotation deformers (the leg IK angle, and the tail and wing angles), and the skinned meshes (the bend of the joint band). A leg pose stacked on a leg angle that was turned by hand, such as the ankle, still has the error above (for example a full crouch and then the ankle turned 40° puts the toe off by about 10 px). Among the preset motions only LegKick stacks that way, and the error is about 2.5 px.
- **Leg pose**: crouch and weight shift are solved from the hip position at every key value. Each leg in the leg warp bends by two-bone IK of the hip, the knee, and the ankle. The leg keeps the art’s sideways bend. The extra bend faces the viewer, so from the front the thigh shortens instead of swinging outward, and it then turns inward or outward by the pose. The foot row stays fixed. When a sideways hip shift means the leg cannot reach the art’s straight length, the hips sink automatically until both feet can reach. A leg bone with a bound mesh solves its rotation with two-bone IK from the hip position the runtime actually has. The knee bends outward (inward for knees-in), and the foot stays level. Body X/Y is carried by the stance warp under the leg bone: a rotation deformer cannot pass down the leg warp’s bend, so each leg bone that has a mesh hanging on it has a warp whose axes are body X/Y (3 keys each). The control points at each key value are solved backward from the bone position the runtime actually has, so the mesh lands where the leg warp puts it — the hips move with the body, the knees bend, and the feet do not move, which agrees with an unbound leg. Each pose is solved with the others at 0. When they are in effect together they only add, and the feet stay still only when one of the amounts is very small. Preset motions therefore never drive crouch and weight shift at the same time.
- **Export requirement**: a blend shape on a rotation deformer needs Cubism 5.0 or newer. When the export target is below 5.0, every bone parameter is baked into keyforms of an ordinary parameter. Poses on rotation deformers are removed (export says so).

## Limits

- **A mesh that combines left and right is not bound.** After import, a limb layer should be split into one mesh on each side with Model → Check separate meshes of the whole model…. If both legs or both feet are still drawn on one layer, automatic inference builds the bones of both legs, but it does not bind that mesh, and it does not generate crouch and weight shift. Split first, then bind them separately in Edit skeleton.
- **A mesh whose topology was edited by hand is not refined.** A mesh with a hand topology edit in history (subdivide, mesh points, and so on) replays that edit by vertex index, so the skeleton does not insert vertices into it. If a bend is not smooth enough, add points at the joint by hand.
- When a flexible bone chain is very wide on both sides (for example a wide fin), the inside folds where the bend radius is smaller than the half-width. The joint limits can be reduced, or the wide part can be split into a branch. A sharp bend in the art that already folds back on itself (a U whose width is greater than the turn radius) is the same: a vertex on the inside corner is on the other side of the center of curvature, and it flips when the bend opens or continues.
- After straightening, a non-flexible child bone hung under a flexible bone chain is still skinned by the rest geometry. It does not unfold with the chain.
- The precision of a mesh that uses nested warp deformers is limited by the mesh spacing. When neighbouring parts that are not connected are only a few pixels apart, and they sit on a sharp bend, the drawn pose can be off by a few pixels. A hairpin bend smaller than half the band width (for example a U that is 16 px wide with only 4 px in the middle) can be off by more than ten pixels.
- Straighten-and-fit has only as many degrees of freedom as there are bones. When a few bones have to go around a large bend (for example three segments around 200°), the center line can still ripple by a few pixels after it is unfolded. Subdivide the bones first, then straighten. The fit moves joints, the mesh’s joint refinement is cut again, and the vertex count can change.
- In a project saved by an earlier version, if a later regeneration merge never reparented the tail mesh, later merges keep treating it as “reparented by the user”, and skeleton changes (including straighten) no longer affect it. The quality report then shows `REGENERATION_CONFLICT` and a “can be repaired” note. Running Update generated results once restores it.
- Length after straightening is unfolded by bone length. The curve is slightly shorter than the bone polyline at a turn, so the more sharply the art bends, the slightly longer it is after unfolding (four bones around 90° are about 3% longer).
- After straightening, hand-painted weights are still sampled at the drawn positions, but grabbing or painting weights in the editor reads the straightened rest position, so the two are offset. A straightened bone chain should use automatic weights.
- A bend past about 90° overlaps on the inside of the joint, the same as Spine’s linear skinning. Improving it depends on adjusting the joint band width, or on splitting the art.
- After the skeleton is baked, Cubism Editor does not keep the bone meaning. To keep editing the bones, keep this application’s project file.
