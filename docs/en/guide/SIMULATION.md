# Simulation and baking

[Documentation](../README.md) · [Physics](PHYSICS.md) · [Swing generation](SWING.md) · [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md) · [MCP usage and API](../agent/MCP_AUTHORING.md)

For a first pass, see the [simulation tutorial](SIMULATION_TUTORIAL.md) and follow the screenshots through presets, pin weights, baking again, and checking the result.

Simulation runs a real 2D cloth / hair simulation on meshes inside the editor: vertices are held by pins, and gravity and inertia drive them. The Cubism runtime does not run this kind of simulation, so **the simulation itself is not exported**. To export it, **bake** it into something Cubism can play: mesh keyforms on new parameters, driven by a fitted pendulum, plus exact corrections on several more parameters. The bake is stored in the project. Rebuilding the model only writes the result back. It does not simulate again.

## Basic workflow

1. Select a skirt or hair mesh on the canvas. In the Simulation panel (in every workspace it sits after the Physics tab; the edit workspace shows it by default) click New, and choose Cloth or Hair. Every mesh in the same body is simulated together.
2. Name the pins. Either or both of these can be used:
   - In Vertex groups on the panel, click Paint on the Pin row, or switch the canvas to Simulate mode and paint the Pin weight group with the weight brush.
   - In Glue on the panel, set glue that joins the waist to Pin.
3. Turn on the preview switch on the toolbar to switch to Reference sim. Turn the head and shake the body in the preview, and adjust the material until the motion looks natural.
4. In the Bake section of the selected body (the first section of the editor) click Bake, or click Bake all on the toolbar at the top of the panel. A bake takes several seconds and runs in the background. Other edits can continue meanwhile. When it finishes, it is committed as its own undo step on the latest version at that moment. Bake automatically on the toolbar is off by default. With it on, each change in the panel commits at once, and a rebake follows in the background. After the bake, the Physics panel gains a pendulum group whose source is Simulation, the parameter panel gains `ParamSim…` parameters, and export includes them.
5. Turn the preview switch off to return to Export. What the preview plays is then the exported model’s motion, to compare with the reference simulation.

### Panel layout

The panel uses the same layout as the Physics panel:

- **Toolbar**: one row holds New (cloth / hair), Bake all, the Bake automatically switch, and the preview switch (off is Export, on is Reference sim, and the label shows which one is current). The next row is the bake status. On the right are expand all and collapse all (the body list and each section of the editor). While the reference simulation is on, the far right adds a reset button that puts the live simulation back at rest. New creates a body from the selected meshes. The name is `sim` plus the first mesh’s name with the `ArtMesh` prefix removed (for example `ArtMeshBottomwear` → `simBottomwear`; a duplicate adds a number). Bake all bakes every enabled body that is unbaked or stale, in order. A later body bakes on top of the earlier bake results, and when all of them finish they are committed as one undo step. Clicking it again when everything is already current rebakes all of them. Editing can continue while a bake runs, and other bodies can be baked too (they queue for the next round). When a body’s settings change during its bake, its result is not committed from the old settings. It is baked again. A body deleted during a bake discards its result. Bake automatically is a local interface preference. It is not saved with the project, and it is off by default. To the right of the buttons, a status count is shown: while something is waiting to bake it shows “1/2 need baking” in yellow, and otherwise “2/2 baked”. During a bake the button becomes cancel (cancel commits no result), and the status shows the current body, its index, and progress. When one body fails to bake, its old bake is kept and a note appears under the list. The rest are committed as usual.
- **List**: a collapsible Bodies folder. The checkbox on the left of each row enables or disables the body. On the right are the bake status and the type. A baked body shows only a green dot. A status that needs attention is written out (yellow Stale means export still uses the old result, red Not baked means it will not be exported, grey Off). The context menu can bake, clear the bake, or delete.
- **Editor**: the title row is the name and the type (switching applies that type’s material preset). Then, in order, Bake, Material, Inputs, Outputs, Glue, and Vertex groups. The Bake section first shows a visual check on motions it has not seen (swing ratio, lag, settling, jitter, shown in the warning color when a difference can be seen), then R² and the error (a separate warning when a value hits an end or the motion is jerkier), then Bake / Bake again, Clear bake, the mode count, the up/down parameter, keys, and exaggeration. How the modes are written, the motions that take part in training, exact poses, and each mode’s swing are folded under Advanced. The Compare section (collapsed by default) puts the bake and the reference simulation together. See [Export and reference simulation](#export-and-reference-simulation). The top of the Material section can choose a named preset. After the material changes, a Back to the preset of this kind button appears on the section title. The Inputs section lists each parameter’s name and ID in its own row. A parameter that no longer exists in the model is marked missing in the warning color. After a bake, each input shows underneath how far it moves the body. One that was dropped is marked Left out in the warning color, with a Keep checkbox. The row below that is its training range. When the inputs differ from the defaults, a Reset to the default inputs button appears on the section title. With no inputs, a warning says it cannot be baked. The Outputs section, after a bake, lists the generated mode parameters and pendulums (name and ID). Click a name to rename it. Clearing it, or changing it back to the default name, restores the name taken from the body. Under each mode parameter, the ID, the range, and the gain can be changed.

## Vertex weight groups

A weight group is a per-vertex weight from 0 to 1 on each mesh. It exists only in this application, and it is not written into `.moc3` / `.cmo3`. Each mesh has one group per kind. They are told apart by kind, and they do not need names. They are painted in Simulate mode with the weight brush or the weight gradient (see [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md)). The left toolbar lists each kind under the weight tools (`1`–`5` switches them too). Clicking one switches the group to paint. The weight brush can also use a circle, line, or rectangle tip. After a topology edit they are kept by interpolating from where the vertices came from.

| Kind | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Pin (`1`) | Vertices held by the rig. The weight is how firmly they are pinned |
| Stiffness (`2`) | Scales stretch and bend stiffness per vertex, for a stiff root and a soft hem |
| Shape (`3`) | Scales the spring back toward the drawn shape, per vertex |
| Mass (`4`) | Scales mass per vertex |
| Damping (`5`) | Scales damping per vertex |

Vertex groups on the panel lists whether each kind has been painted on the target meshes. Paint on a row selects the first target mesh, switches the canvas to Simulate mode, and opens the weight brush for that kind. Force fields such as wind are not implemented yet. The panel and the weight brush do not offer a Wind group. MCP’s `vertex_group_update` / `vertex_group_paint` still accept `wind`, and it takes effect only when `simulation_simulate` is given `wind`. MCP can still use named groups and name them in the body’s `groups`. When one kind has several groups and none is named, the first is used, which is the one the interface paints.

## Glue

Glue does not become a pin on its own. Each glue connected to a simulated mesh is chosen separately in the panel:

| Role | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Ignore (default) | The simulation pays no attention to it. It is only welded at runtime |
| Pin | The glue vertices on the simulation side follow the other mesh’s actual deformation, for example a skirt and the waist |
| Hold together | When both sides are in the simulation, the seam stays joined, for example separate front and back pieces |

## Material

| Item | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Mass | Relative density. Each vertex’s mass is computed from the mesh area assigned to it (one third of the area of the surrounding triangles), so refining or re-tessellating the mesh only adds deformation detail. It does not make the cloth heavier or softer |
| Stretch | How strongly it resists growing longer. Cloth and hair should be near 1 |
| Bend | How sharply it may bend: the difference in angle between neighbouring triangles. It handles bending only, not shrinking |
| Area | How firmly each triangle keeps its area. Seen from the front, cloth shortens and bunches when it folds in depth. A low value lets light cloth shrink that way. A high value keeps a heavy part in shape. However it is set, a triangle does not flip or collapse to zero area |
| Grain | How much softer it is across the grain than along it. The grain is taken automatically as the direction away from the pins (the direction in which path length measured along the mesh increases). With no pins, it is straight down. At a high value a strand does not stretch along its length, but it can part sideways and fold along its length |
| Damping | How fast motion dies out (per second) |
| Shape | A spring back toward the drawn shape. 0 is pure physics. It is counted per unit mass, so mesh density does not change how fast it springs back |
| Slack | During a hard throw, how far past the rest path a vertex may get from its pin |

Defaults: cloth has area 0.3 and grain 0.3. Hair has area 0.5 and grain 0.7. The presets for sleeves, trousers, and legwear use area 0.5. Neckwear uses 0.4.

The Preset dropdown fills the items above with the values of a named material, and they can still be adjusted afterward. The project stores only the values. When the values match a preset, the dropdown shows that preset’s name. Otherwise it shows Custom.

| Type | Preset | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloth | Cotton (default) | The defaults in the table above |
| Cloth | Silk | Light and soft, fine folds, a long swing, and a slow stop |
| Cloth | Chiffon | The lightest and softest. Air damping stops it sooner than silk |
| Cloth | Wool | Heavy and soft. It starts late and stops quickly |
| Cloth | Denim | Heavy and stiff. Wide folds, and it keeps its shape |
| Cloth | Leather | The stiffest. It swings as a whole, barely bunches, and springs back to the drawn shape |
| Cloth | Elastic | Can stretch and spring back. Suits bands and socks |
| Hair | Hair (default) | The defaults in the table above |
| Hair | Fine hair | Light, easy to bend and to part, and it swings for a long time |
| Hair | Thick hair | Heavier. The whole strand swings and keeps its shape |
| Hair | Bangs | Lies on the forehead instead of hanging down. A stronger spring-back and more damping. It moves with the head, and the tips lag slightly. The front-hair preset uses this |

Before the simulation starts it is calibrated, so the drawn shape is the rest equilibrium. A model that is still does not sag when the simulation is turned on.

The solver is 2D XPBD (16 substeps per frame). When the rig stretches somewhere (for example parallax during a head turn), that place’s rest length grows with it, and it does not fight the rig’s shape. When the rig shortens somewhere, that is treated as turning into depth, and the soft compression takes it.

## Baking

Inputs are the parameters that shake the body. They are used as the list stands. There is no implied default. Creating a body (the panel, MCP with no `inputs`, or a model preset) writes the default inputs: the head and body angles that exist in the model (`ParamAngleX`, `ParamAngleZ`, `ParamBodyAngleX`, `ParamBodyAngleZ`), plus nodding, the body moving up and down, and leaning (the existing `ParamAngleY`, `ParamBodyAngleY`, `ParamBodyLean`). A cloth type’s up/down input takes only `ParamBodyAngleY`: the clothes hang on the body, and a nod does not move them. Leaning mostly leaves them at another pose. An up/down pendulum cannot express a static offset, and it would be fitted as a pendulum with almost no restoring force, amplified by a huge output scale. Applying the clothing preset again changes a body that still has the old shared default inputs over to the cloth default inputs. An empty list means there are no inputs, and the bake reports an error. A body in an old project that recorded no inputs (which meant “use the defaults” then) opens as the full default list above. Parameters the model does not have are removed the next time that body is edited. The default inputs are treated as the same as the “empty” of that time, so an existing bake does not go stale because of it.

A bake first reads from the rig how each input moves the body. An input that moves no vertex by 1 px inside the training range does not take part in the bake (the panel marks it Left out; checking Keep, or MCP `force_inputs`, can force it to stay). An input that moves the body up and down more than left and right goes into the up/down parameters (see below). The rest go into the left/right modes. So a parameter in the default inputs that has nothing to do with that part (for example a head turn on a skirt) is no longer fitted into the pendulum, and the body does not shake for no reason when the user moves those parameters.

Each input can set a training range (low end to high end, in parameter units). The bake drives it only inside that range. The default is the parameter’s whole range. The range always contains the default value, and the part past the parameter’s range is clamped. Mode parameters are scaled to the strongest motion in the training (see step 4), so a narrower range swings more fully inside the range, and it hits the ends sooner past the range. For example, if `ParamBodyAngleX` usually stays within ±5, setting the training range to ±5 lets ordinary motions use the full mode parameter too. After the training range changes, the bake is stale. When an input is removed, its training range and its keep setting are removed with it.

Train on motions (under Advanced, MCP `training_clips`) makes the bake learn the model’s own motions as well, beyond the designed excitation (user motions, or built-in Idle, Nod, Shake, and so on). Each is weighted from ×0.05 to ×1. It suits a model driven mostly by a few fixed motions, with no face tracking. Each motion counts only toward the parameter group it moves the most. After those motions are edited, bake again (the motion contents are not part of the stale check).

Outputs are the mode parameters and pendulums the bake writes. Names default to the body’s name (parameters such as “<name>”, “<name> 2”, and “<name> Y”, and a pendulum with the same name as the body). A rename is stored on the body and applied on rebuild. It does not need another bake, and the ID stays the same. Each mode parameter can also set the ID, the range, and the gain it is written with, and that likewise does not need another bake. The ID defaults to the generated `ParamSim<id>_<k>`. It can be changed to another ID, to reuse motions and physics made for that ID, and it cannot share a name with another parameter in the model or with this body’s inputs. The range is ±1…±100, ±30 by default. The keys and the pendulum’s output scale stretch with it, and the motion stays the same. The gain is ×0…×3, and it defaults to the exaggeration. 0 is still. The bake result itself is always stored under the generated ID and ±30. These settings are applied on rebuild. Renaming a pendulum whose source is Simulation in the Physics panel writes here too. The pendulum is still driven by the bake.

### Settings

| Item | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Modes (1–3, default 2) | How many parameters are generated. Each is driven by one vertex of the fitted pendulum. The first mode is the overall swing. Each later mode is one more segment hung below, or an independent pendulum, and it only fills in what the modes above cannot show: the body bending, bunching, and compressing as it lags. With only 1 mode the shape can only move as a whole with the swing angle, and the cloth’s compression is missing, so the default is 2. A long strand or a ribbon can use 3 |
| Up/down (Auto / On / Off) | Whether the up/down inputs are baked on their own into `ParamSim<id>_Y` (see below). Auto generates it only when they move the body by 2 px or more. On always generates it. Off leaves the up/down inputs out of the bake, and it reports an error when the only inputs are up/down |
| Keys (3 / 5 / 7 / 9) | How many keys each mode parameter and each exact-pose parameter has, spread evenly. More keys follow an arc more closely. What increases is keyforms, not parameters |
| Exaggeration (×1–×2, default ×1.3) | How much larger the mode swing is than the simulation. It takes effect when the keyforms are written back. Changing it acts at once, does not need another bake, and does not push the parameters to their ends. An anime style usually looks better a little larger than real |
| Exact poses | Parameters baked as exact corrections on their own axes (at most four). None are baked by default |
| Modes as | Auto (default), Blend shapes, or Keyform axes. Both ways play the same. Keyform axes can be read by every Cubism version, but each mode multiplies the mesh’s keyform count by its key count. Blend shapes (Cubism 4.2+) add their keyforms on top of the existing keyforms. Auto uses blend shapes only when some target mesh’s keyforms would exceed 64. When the target version does not support them, they are always written as keyform axes |

What blend shapes save is keyforms, not parameters. For example, a mesh that originally has 9 keyforms, with 2 modes of 5 keys each, is 9 × 5 × 5 = 225 as keyform axes, and 9 plus 8 blend shapes as blend shapes.

### Steps

1. **Exact poses**: the rig is posed to each key of each exact-pose parameter, the body is left to settle, and the difference from the rig is written as a correction keyform on that parameter’s axis. This step is skipped when no exact-pose parameter is named.
2. **Drive one input at a time**: each input that takes part in the bake is driven on its own, and the other inputs stay at their defaults. One stretch turns to the high end of the training range within 0.15 seconds, holds until the body is still (1.6 seconds at most), returns to the default, turns to the low end, and comes back. Another stretch is a log sweep from 0.25 Hz to 3 Hz, with the amplitude falling off near 3 Hz. Each stretch starts from rest, and they are simulated in parallel on their own copies of the body. The left/right group and the up/down group share these simulations. Each frame records the difference between the body and the rig (plus the exact-pose corrections), converted into each mesh’s own keyform coordinates. Each group also has a stretch in which this group’s inputs are dragged, shaken, and left to sway together, and that stretch is used only for the check. Each stretch is weighted evenly by how much it moves (at most a factor of 100 apart), so an input that moves the body only a little still has to be fitted correctly, and a large throw does not drown it out.
   Previously every input moved at random inside one shared stretch, and the fit could not tell which input had moved the body. On `tml` back hair, `ParamBodyAngleX` moves the hair root by about 70 px, but the pendulum gave it only 1% weight, and the exported swing while following a mouse drag was only about two tenths of the simulation. After driving one input at a time, each input gets a weight that moves the body the way that input does.
3. **Pendulum**: one Cubism pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>` is fitted, with as many segments as there are modes, and the modes are fitted one by one. First only the segment of the first mode is fitted. Then each later mode is fitted, and each mode fits only the remaining motion the modes above cannot read, inside the principal direction of that remaining motion (not the principal direction of the motion as a whole). At the end every segment and every weight is fine-tuned together for one round. Readout is always solved one mode at a time, in mode order. A later mode has two kinds of candidate: hang one more segment under the pendulum (a pendulum vertex is influenced only by the vertices above it, so adding a segment does not change the modes above, and the search starts from 4 starts), or use an independent one-segment pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>_<k>`, with its own inputs, pendulum length, and reaction speed (compression may react to acceleration faster and stiffer than a swing). Whichever is better on the check motions is used. The parameter count stays the same. Fitting them together makes neighbouring vertices’ swing angles almost synchronous, the two modes’ shapes grow large and cancel each other, and motions outside the training then go wrong. The score looks at the overall result, not only the error: how well it follows the simulation, how far it throws and swings back after the input stops (counted once more on its own), and whether each stretch of motion is jerkier than the simulation. Each input’s type (X translation or angle) and its initial weight are estimated by least squares of a small stack (when there are no more than 6 inputs, every combination of types is compared, and the one with the smallest remaining error is taken). Nelder–Mead then searches, together, each segment’s pendulum length, reaction speed, sway, settling, and each input’s weight. Every candidate is scored by playing all of the training motions at their real amplitude, with the same computation as export. Among the candidates, the one with the best score on the check motions wins.
4. **Mode parameters**: the angle of pendulum vertex k is written to the parameter `ParamSim<id>_<k>`, from −30 to 30. The output scale is set from the pendulum’s largest swing angle across every training motion and check motion, with another 10% of headroom, so the parameter does not hit ±30. When a parameter sits at an end the hair stops, and that reads as a hitch. The parameter range is ±30 rather than ±1 because a keyform axis snaps a value within 0.001 of a key onto that key (the Cubism runtime does this too). Inside a range of ±1, a large body hitches on that snap every time it swings through the rest position.
5. **Keyforms**: each mode is solved by least squares. Each mode’s keyform fits the shape left after the modes before it have explained what they can. The shape of each frame equals the sum of each parameter’s keyform interpolation at the value the pendulum plays. Keyforms are constrained to a smooth curve. They do not turn a sharp corner at neighbouring keys, and the line passes straight through the rest key. Otherwise the speed jumps when the parameter sweeps across a key, which is another kind of hitch. Two adjustments are then made on the check motions, and they are kept only when the follow is better and the motion is not jerkier: each mode’s keyform is solved for another round after the other modes are subtracted (closer to solving them together, and they still do not cancel), and a later mode tries gains of ×0.25, ×0.5, and ×0.75, so it does not add noise on a small motion. A parameter that barely moves is not generated.

**Up/down is baked on its own**: a Cubism pendulum hangs along gravity and outputs an angle only. When the root moves straight up and down, the angle does not change, so up/down motion cannot drive it no matter how the inputs are set. The approach matches an artist’s `裙y` and `长发y` physics groups. Inputs that move the body up and down more than left and right (usually nodding, the body moving up and down, and leaning) are separated from the other inputs, and steps 3–5 are run once more on their own: one single-segment pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>_y` is fitted, it drives one mode parameter `ParamSim<id>_Y` (named “<simulation name> Y”), and the keyforms are the shape of the body’s up/down lag and spring-back. The two groups’ keyforms add on the mesh. When the up/down motion is under 2 px, this parameter is not generated (Up/down set to On always generates it; set to Off, it is not generated and the up/down inputs do not take part in the bake). When the only inputs are up/down, every mode is fitted this way. `tml` skirt: the residual in the simulation during a crouch / stand is at most 47 px, and the old bake left only a static tilt. After the separate bake, R² on the check motions rose from 0.37 to 0.61.

R² is not the only standard for whether it looks right. When the swing is a bit small and the phase is a bit off, least squares shrinks the keyforms as a whole to buy a lower error, and the exported motion then looks lifeless. So after a bake the panel shows a visual check of each group on the check motions first:

| Measure | Meaning | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swing | The swing of the tip (the tenth of the vertices that move the most) along the simulation’s principal direction, as the ratio of export to simulation | Outside ×0.75–×1.3 |
| Lag | How many milliseconds the export is behind the simulation (the cross-correlation peak) | Over 80 ms |
| Settling | How long it takes to return to rest after the input stops, as the ratio of export to simulation | Outside ×0.6–×1.6 |
| Jitter | The square root of the ratio of third-difference energy | Over ×1.5 |

The opposite direction warns as well. MCP also returns direction agreement, the difference in swing-back count, the fraction of frames that hit an end or got stuck, how much the strands shortened, and the deviation at rest. The next row is R² and the maximum deviation from the simulation of 95% of the frames. When a frame hits an end, or the jerk exceeds 1.5 times the simulation, another warning row is added. Each mode parameter’s swing is under Advanced. MCP `simulation_bake` returns every measure and each input’s read result (`inputs`).

A baked pendulum group can be viewed, edited, or turned off in the Physics panel, the same as an ordinary generated group. An edit in the Physics panel is an override of the generated pendulum. After a rebake, or after an output’s ID or range is changed, the override is carried onto the new pendulum by a three-way merge: only the fields you changed are kept (an output scale is kept as its ratio to the original value), and the rest takes the newly fitted values. Outputs follow the parameter rename. An override that is identical to the new pendulum after the change is removed. When the bake is cleared, the body is deleted, or a new bake no longer generates some pendulum, its override, disabled state, and order are removed together.

### Quality and time

Bodies generated by the model presets (2 modes, 5 keys, exaggeration ×1) were compared, frame by frame, between the reference simulation and the exported model (Cubism pendulum + keyforms) on 13–15 stretches of motion that did not appear during the bake: mouse follow like face tracking, head and body leaning left and right, the built-in Idle / Nod / Shake, a step of each input, a head turn, a drag, and a shake. Only motions whose tip moves 10 px or more in the simulation are counted (the stretch count is in parentheses). The other motions are driven only by inputs that were dropped. In the simulation they are only a slight shake as the body starts, and counting them would pull every measure toward 0. Direction agrees is the number of motions whose swing direction agrees (cosine ≥ 0.9). Swing is the factor converted back from the median of how far each motion’s export-to-simulation ratio departs from 1. R² is the mean of the motions (a value below −1 is counted as −1). Rest deviation is the distance between export and simulation when the input has been still for 1 second or more (the 95th percentile). The earlier column is the old result trained on the motion library. The later column drives one input at a time.

| Part | Direction agrees | Mean R² | Swing | Rest deviation | Bake time |

| tml back hair | 5 → 7 / 11 | -0.03 → 0.48 | ×0.43 → ×0.72 | 10.5 → 10.3 px | 20.3¹ → 5.7 s |
| tml back hair (top 10% pinned) | 6 → 6 / 10 | 0.14 → 0.54 | ×0.32 → ×0.75 | 12.2 → 9.7 px | 7.6 → 6.5 s |
| tml front hair (bangs) | 8 → 8 / 13 | 0.11 → 0.51 | ×0.34 → ×0.83 | 6.2 → 5.1 px | 3.5 → 2.5 s |
| tml skirt | 3 → 3 / 5 | 0.35 → 0.30 | ×0.84 → ×0.84 | 3.2 → 3.2 px | 3.9 → 1.5 s |
| ds back hair | 4 → 8 / 11 | 0.21 → 0.46 | ×0.14 → ×0.66 | 7.0 → 5.8 px | 7.3 → 5.1 s |
| ds back hair (top 10% pinned) | 4 → 8 / 10 | 0.25 → 0.56 | ×0.18 → ×0.71 | 5.9 → 5.9 px | 5.4 → 4.0 s |
| ds front hair (bangs) | 5 → 8 / 11 | 0.23 → 0.54 | ×0.25 → ×0.69 | 3.3 → 2.0 px | 4.1 → 2.4 s |

- Hair improves the most, and it is especially clear on motions close to real use. `tml` back hair following the mouse goes from 0.19 times the simulation’s swing to 1.04 times, and R² from −0.15 to 0.92. The Shake motion’s R² goes from 0.07 to 0.83. A `ParamBodyAngleX` step goes from not moving at all to R² 0.86.
- Cloth bakes 2–4 times faster after driving one input at a time, but R² is worse than before (skirts and tops around 0.3, previously 0.35–0.5). The up/down parameter is still weak (a `ParamBodyAngleY` step has R² 0.17–0.30 and a swing of 0.2–0.8 times): in the simulation the hem throws to the same side both when the body moves up and when it moves down, and the return also stays on one side, while a pendulum’s output is symmetric in the sign of the input, so the fit can only compromise.
- Back hair’s swing period for the body leaning left and right (`ParamBodyAngleZ`, about 1.3 seconds) differs from a head turn (about 0.9 seconds), and the shape differs too. With 2 modes it cannot be separated out (ds back hair’s export barely moves; tml back hair has a swing of 0.59 times and R² 0.11). Switching to 3 modes does not improve it either.
- An input that barely moves the cloth, such as a head turn (the simulation moves only about 2 px), is dropped outright. The skirt does not shake for no reason when the user turns the head.

The times were measured on a 32-thread desktop. Simulation and fitting run in parallel across cores, and fewer cores are slower. The table above is generated by the development tool `SimBakeSuite`, which writes every visual measure of each motion stretch and the tip-trajectory plots. Usage is in [Development tools](DEVELOPMENT.md#development-tools).

### Stale bakes

A bake records the setup it was based on, the target meshes, the weight groups, the related glue, and the solver version (after the solver changes, an old project’s bake is shown as stale). After they change, the panel says the bake is stale. With automatic baking on in the toolbar, a change committed in the panel is baked again in the background (as its own undo step), and the search starts from the previous pendulum, which is faster than baking from scratch. Applying a model preset likewise commits first, then bakes the bodies it generated in the background. MCP `simulation_put` decides by the body’s own `auto_bake` (on by default). A change made somewhere else (a mesh, weights) still shows as stale. A stale bake is still exported. Bake again updates it. Clear bake removes its parameters, keyforms, and pendulum. When automatic baking fails or is cancelled, the old bake is kept and the reason is shown. When a target mesh is rebuilt (the vertex count changes), that mesh’s bake result is skipped and the panel says so.

### Export and reference simulation

The top of the panel, and the toolbar of the canvas Simulate mode, can switch what the preview shows:

- **Export**: the preview plays the bake, the parameters and keyforms driven by the fitted Cubism pendulum, which matches the exported model’s motion. An unbaked body does not move here, and the bottom left of the canvas says there is an unbaked simulation.
- **Reference sim**: the canvas shows the editor’s own physics simulation. It is only for comparison, and it is never exported. The bottom left of the canvas marks Reference sim · not exported in the warning color. That body’s own `ParamSim…` parameters stay still then, so the motion is not added twice.

The Compare section of a body’s editor puts the two together:

- **View** (available while the reference simulation is on): how the reference simulation is shown on the first preview canvas — Reference (in place of the export, as above), Export (the export only), or Overlay (a half-transparent reference simulation drawn on top of the export, and only the simulated layers are drawn). Each canvas remembers its own choice, and it is saved with the workspace.
- **Side by side**: turns the reference simulation on, and makes sure there are two preview canvases. The first shows the export, the second shows the reference, and both follow the same pose and play at the same time.
- **Play**: choose a motion from the dropdown (the built-in Idle, Nod, and Shake, plus skeleton motions when there are bones, and user motions). The reference simulation and the exported pendulum both return to rest, then play it, so the stretches can be compared.
- **Check**: in the background, drives the reference simulation and the exported model separately with the chosen motion (gain ×1), and gives the same visual check as the Bake section. MCP’s counterpart is `simulation_compare`.

## Model presets

The Physics & simulation group of the Model presets panel (formerly Model settings) turns common parts into a simulation and a bake in one step. The result is ordinary weight groups and bodies, which can still be adjusted in the Simulation panel afterward. The three groups in the panel, Deformation & mouth, Motions, and Physics & simulation, are all collapsible rows in the same style as the parameter panel’s folders, with a summary on the right. The Motions group only decides whether the model has the Basic motions and Skeleton motions groups. A single motion is enabled and adjusted in the motion panel. Physics & simulation is one row per item. Front hair and back hair each have a dropdown, switching among Simulate / Classic sway / Off. Clothing and eye jelly are checkboxes. Checking applies the preset, and clearing removes it. The per-mesh looseness list is shown under the clothing row. Grey text beside a control describes the current contents (which clothing parts are simulated, or that the art has no such part; hover shows the full text). When it is unbaked, or the body has been deleted, the note uses the warning color. Bake progress and cancel are in the status bar at the bottom. A failure reason is shown at the bottom of the group. Each check is one undo step. MCP’s counterpart is the `model_apply_preset` tool.

The Parts & modules group of the same panel lists the modules that generate the rig automatically, by part (head and face, eyes, brows, mouth and lower face, hair, body, clothing and accessories, expressions, motions). Each card covers both sides. Each module is one row, with a checkbox. The grey text on the right is the parameters, keyforms, and physics groups it adds for the current art (an estimate while it is off, marked with “≈”). Once it is on, its few adjustment values are shown. Only modules that apply to the current art are listed. The presets at the top (Full / Standard / Minimal) switch them by importance in one step. The parameter budget turns modules off, least important and most parameters first, until the added parameters do not exceed it. The button on the right restores the defaults. A new project has everything on by default. A project saved before modules were introduced still generates as it did, and the top of the group says it can be upgraded. Each change is one undo step. MCP uses `module_catalog` to view it and `changes.rig` of `settings_update` to change it. See [Rig preset architecture](../spec/RIG_PRESETS.md).

Generate from PSD on the Start page (the far left of the workspace tab bar, or File → Start page) gathers the same presets **before** the PSD is imported. Minimal / Default / Full at the top of the property panel are, in order, bindings only (no motions, no physics and simulation, no lip outline), both motion groups plus classic hair sway plus clothing simulation plus eye jelly, and, on top of the default, simulating front and back hair and turning on feature displacement. The items below can be adjusted one by one, and the choice is remembered. After that, whether the PSD is imported from the Start page, the File menu, recent files, or a drop, the changed presets are written as one undo step after generation, then the hair and clothing simulations are generated in order (each simulation is its own step), and the edit workspace is shown. Clothing is on by default. When clothing is recognized in the art, the clothing simulation is generated and baked. When a model generated from a PSD is already open, Apply to the current model in the Generate button’s menu applies the same presets to it. With Check separate meshes on import turned on at the right, a new PSD project checks the layers that can be split first, and the presets are applied after the split is confirmed or the layers are kept as they are. The check can also be started by hand from Model → Check separate meshes of the whole model….

| Preset | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Front hair / back hair | Every mesh of that hair becomes one hair body (`preset_front_hair` / `preset_back_hair`). Each strand (each connected component of a mesh) is pinned at its own top and freed toward the tip, and only the pin group is written. Front hair uses the Bangs material. Back hair uses the default hair material. That hair’s old sway is removed at the same time (the `ParamHairFront/Back` parameters, the `DeformHair*Physics` deformers, and the pendulum). The meshes (including parts that were split out) are reparented to the follow deformer, and the drawn position stays the same. Choosing Classic sway or Off deletes that body and restores the old sway (Off also disables the old sway) |
| Clothing | Reads the top, bottom, neckwear, hands (sleeves), and legwear layers, and simulates only where the art looks loose. Tight parts follow the rig. A bottom is judged as a skirt or trousers first: the name wins (裙, skirt, スカート / 裤, pants, ズボン, and so on). Otherwise the alpha outline is used, and a center-seam gap that continues from the crotch to the hem is judged as trousers. The torso and the bottom are compared with the body outline row by row. The body outline starts at the shoulder line (a bottom starts at the waist line) and may widen only slowly downward. The part that sticks out is recorded as loose (a ruffle, a bow, a flared hem, an open coat). A top below the waist line (the waist line is the bottom’s waist line, or the top of legwear that reaches up under the top), a skirt below the hips and wider than the hips, and neckwear below the knot are treated as hanging free. Straight trousers and a pencil skirt stay tight. Sleeves and legwear bend with the limb, and they use a morphological opening instead: only a part thinner than a disk of half the limb’s thickness (a cuff or sock-top ruffle, a ribbon) counts as loose. Puff sleeves and turned-back cuffs follow the limb. A skin-colored region is always tight, and spread fingers are not simulated either. Looseness grows with the in-layer distance to the tight region, and it generates pins (1 − looseness), mass, and wind (both increase with looseness; a limb gets less wind). A mesh with too few loose vertices builds no body, and it is removed from the clothing-preset body it used to belong to. Bodies are split by part into `preset_top`, `preset_skirt`, `preset_trousers`, `preset_neckwear`, `preset_sleeves`, and `preset_legwear`. Trousers, sleeves, and legwear are stiffer. Under the clothing row, each mesh lists its part and a looseness bar. Tight ones are gathered into one row. Hovering a bottom shows why it was judged that way. Clearing the checkbox deletes every clothing-preset body |
| Eye jelly | Switches the eyes’ jelly pendulum |
| Auto pin weights (toolbar) | Recomputes the weight groups above for the selected layers, or for every mesh that is already simulated, by part type, and rebakes the bodies that use them. It does not create a body |

The groups a preset writes are named `preset_pin`, `preset_mass`, and `preset_wind`. When a body has already named its own groups in `groups`, that group is refreshed in place. Applying the same preset again updates the existing body. It does not stack another one. A mesh that already belongs to a body the user created is refused. The wind group currently takes effect only when MCP `simulation_simulate` is given wind (see above).

## MCP

Simulation is split into several tools by operation. Parameters go in `{"request": {...}}`. Each names the body by `id`, and carries `project_id`, `state`, and `request_id`. They return a background job handle. Take the result with `job_wait` / `job_get`:

- `simulation_put`: creates or partially edits a body by `id`. Fields include `targets`, `material`, `material_preset` (the preset names in the table above: `cotton`, `silk`, `chiffon`, `wool`, `denim`, `leather`, `elastic`, `hair`, `fine_hair`, `thick_hair`, `bangs`; a `material` field given at the same time overrides its values), `groups`, `glue_roles`, `inputs`, `modes`, `keys`, `exaggeration`, `static_inputs`, `blend_shapes` (`true` / `false` / `null` is auto), `auto_bake`, `output_names` (names by parameter or pendulum ID, replaced as a whole), `input_ranges` (a `[min, max]` training range per input parameter, replaced as a whole), `vertical` (`true` / `false` / `null` is auto), `outputs` (`{id, range, gain}` per generated mode-parameter ID, replaced as a whole, and it does not need another bake), `force_inputs` (inputs kept even when they barely move the body), and `training_clips` (motions that take part in training, `{动作: 0.05–1}`, replaced as a whole). When a new body is given no `inputs`, the default inputs are written. An empty array means no inputs. When `auto_bake` is on (the default), it bakes again inside the same step, and returns a `bake` summary or `bake_error`.
- `simulation_simulate`: a read-only trial (the panel has no matching button). Each input is held for `hold` seconds and then released, and `release` seconds are observed. `wind` is optional. It returns the peak, the rest drift, the stretch, and setting hints.
- `simulation_bake`: bakes (several seconds). Returns each mode parameter and its swing, the key count, the pendulum ID and segment count, the IDs of independent pendulums (`own_pendulums`, sometimes), each input’s read result (`inputs`: how far it moves the body, whether it is translation and rotation only, which group it belongs to, or that it was dropped), each group’s visual check on the check motions (`visual`), the bake method (`method`), and, on the check motions, `fit_r2`, `error_p95_px`, `parameter_peak` (the largest fraction of its range a mode parameter uses), `clipped_frames` (the fraction of frames that hit an end), and `jerk_ratio` (jerk compared with the simulation).
- `simulation_compare`: a read-only background job. Drives the reference simulation and the exported model separately with the motions in `motions` (a user motion ID or a built-in name, at most 8), and returns each motion’s visual check.
- `simulation_clear_bake`: clears the bake.
- `simulation_delete`: deletes the body.

The reference-simulation preview is an audition session. It does not enter the document or history. `simulation_preview` starts with `mode=start` and `simulation_id` (`restart` / `stop` carry `session_id`). `simulation_preview_step` advances 1–240 steps by `dt` seconds (at most 0.1). `simulation_preview_get` reads the current frame. `simulation_preview_render` renders the current frame.

`vertex_group_update` generates a weight group by a rule (`fill`, `outline`, `gradient`, `glue`, `region`). `delete=true` deletes it, and no per-vertex array is passed. `vertex_group_paint` changes weights with the same brush, gradient, and invert as the canvas. `workspace_inspect` with `scope=simulations` lists bodies, glue keys, and the bake summary (including `bake_stale`). `scope=vertex_groups` lists the weight groups.

## Current scope

- Implemented: cloth and hair (an XPBD solve, mass assigned by area, triangle area and flip prevention, rotation-based bending, automatic grain and anisotropy, pins, glue roles, and long-range constraints), vertex weight groups, the reference-simulation preview, baking to parameters + keyforms (blend shapes or keyform axes, and it can choose automatically) + a pendulum and exporting them, automatic baking, and the exaggeration factor. Model presets (hair simulation replaces the old sway, tops / skirts / trousers / neckwear / sleeves / legwear are simulated by looseness, and auto pin weights).
- Measured: the comparison data for tml back hair in the table above. Model-preset bakes on tml (check-motion R² / 95% error, measured on the old solver, and they need to be measured again under the new material model): back hair 0.69 / 229 px, the same as pinning the top 10% by hand. Front hair 0.25 / 185 px, and any pin range stays in 0.2–0.3. The front-hair problem is the material, not the pin range. Bangs that sweep across the forehead do not have strand length carrying gravity the way back hair does. The weak spring-back of the default hair material turns them into a soft spring of about 0.75 Hz. Shaking the head left and right (1.2 Hz) leaves them 74 px off the skeleton (the skeleton itself moves 33 px), and a nod leaves them 47 px off. After switching to the Bangs material (the new material model, tml / ds): a head shake is 41 / 30 px, a nod is 12 / 10 px, the leftover after a head turn stops is 10 / 6 px, bake R² is 0.18 → 0.27 / 0.21 → 0.49, and the 95% error is 216 → 85 / 112 → 57 px. The skirt’s data was measured under the collision feature that has since been removed, and it needs to be measured again. Adding mass / wind groups to hair drops back hair’s R² from 0.69 to 0.56, so the hair preset does not write those two groups.
- Obstacles: a body can carry obstacles (`obstacles`), a circle or a capsule that follows another mesh. Particles are kept outside. They take part in the reference simulation and the bake, and they are exported to runtime targets that play a live simulation (p2lrt, the web). They can be written only through MCP `simulation_put` for now. The canvas has no edit entry yet. An earlier mesh collider (by edge distance) measured unreliable, and it has been removed. That kind of collider and the collision weight group in an old project are ignored on load.
- Not implemented yet: exporting force fields such as wind, rigid bodies and soft bodies, and baking to ordinary keys (removing the generator).
- A hand check of the looseness heatmap (tml, ds, and one chibi piece of art): tml is loose only on the lower half of the skirt. ds’s apron, the bow behind the waist, and the hem are loose, and the puff sleeves, cuffs, and sock tops are tight. A chibi open coat is loose below the waist line, and straight trousers are tight.
- Still to be measured: how the example models’ skirts and hair look in the official runtime after export, and how natural the hair and clothing simulations generated by the model presets are.
