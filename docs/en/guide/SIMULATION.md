# Simulation and baking

[Documentation](../../README.md) · [中文](../../zh/guide/SIMULATION.md) · [Physics](PHYSICS.md) · [Swing generation](SWING.md) · [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md) · [MCP usage and interface](../agent/MCP_AUTHORING.md)

For a first run, start with the [illustrated simulation tutorial](SIMULATION_TUTORIAL.md): it walks through the presets, pin weights, baking again and checking the result with screenshots.

Simulation runs a real 2D cloth / hair simulation on meshes in the editor: vertices are held by pins and driven by gravity and inertia. The Cubism runtime does no such simulation, so **the simulation itself is not exported**. To export it, it has to be **baked** into content Cubism can play: mesh keyforms on new parameters, driven by a fitted pendulum, plus exact corrections on a few parameters. The bake is stored in the project; rebuilding the model writes back only the result and does not simulate again.

## Basic workflow

1. Select the skirt or hair meshes on the canvas, then choose **Simulation · Cloth** or **Simulation · Hair** from the **Add** menu on the **Object** page of the **Modifiers** panel. Several meshes in one body simulate together; the body becomes a simulation card in their stacks.
2. Set the pins. Use either way, or both:
   - in the simulation card's **Vertex groups**, click **Paint** on the **Pin** row, or switch the canvas to **Simulate** mode, and paint the **Pin** group with the weight brush;
   - in the card's **Glue**, set the glue joining the waist to **Pin**.
3. Turn on **Reference sim** in the card's **Compare** fold, turn the head and shake the body in the preview, and adjust the material until it looks natural.
4. Click **Bake** in the card's **Bake** fold, or **Bake all** in the **Simulations** section of the Modifiers panel's **World** page. A bake takes a few seconds and runs in the background; you can keep editing other things meanwhile, and it is committed as a separate undo step on whatever the latest version is then. **Bake automatically** is off by default; when on, every change commits at once and then bakes again in the background. After a bake, the Physics panel shows a pendulum group whose origin is **Simulation**, the Parameters panel shows `ParamSim…` parameters, and exports include them.
5. Turn **Reference sim** off to go back to **Export**: the preview now plays the exported model's motion, to compare with the reference simulation.

### Interface

Simulation has no panel of its own; it lives in the **Modifiers** panel:

- **Simulation card** (Object page, in the target meshes' stack): the title is the name, with the bake state (Baked / Bake stale / Not baked), a switch and a menu (bake, apply as keyforms, move up or down, delete) on the right. The card starts with the kind (switching it applies that kind's material preset), then the folds:
  - **Bake**: first the visual check on unseen motion (swing ratio, lag, settling, jitter, in warning colour when the difference shows), then R² and the error (with separate warnings for hitting the ends or extra jitter), **Bake / Bake again**, **Clear bake** and each mode's swing, then Modes, Up/down, Keys and Exaggeration; Modes as, Train on motions and Exact poses sit under **Advanced**.
  - **Material**: named presets at its top; once the material changes, a **Back to the preset of this kind** button appears in its title.
  - **Inputs**: each parameter's name and ID, marking parameters no longer in the model as **missing** in warning colour; after a bake, each input shows below it how far it moves the body, and left-out ones are marked **Left out** in warning colour with a **Keep** checkbox; the row below that is its training range. When the inputs differ from the default, a **Reset to the default inputs** button appears in its title, and with no inputs it warns that it cannot bake.
  - **Outputs**: after a bake, the mode parameters and pendulums made (name and ID); click a name to rename it, and clearing it or setting the default name back returns to naming after the body. Each mode parameter takes an ID, range and gain below it.
  - **Glue**, **Vertex groups**: see below.
  - **Compare**: the **Reference sim** switch (off is **Export**), with a reset button on the fold's title during the reference simulation, and the tools that put the bake beside the reference simulation; see [Export and reference simulation](#export-and-reference-simulation).
- **Simulations section of the World page**: one row holds **Bake all**, **Bake automatically** and a count ("1/2 need baking" in yellow when something waits, otherwise "2/2 baked"), and every body is listed below. The checkbox at the left of each row enables / disables the body; on the right are the bake state and kind: baked shows only a green dot, and only states that need attention are written out (yellow **Stale**, meaning export still uses the old result; red **Not baked**, meaning it does not export; grey **Off**). Click a row to select its mesh and switch to the Object page; the context menu bakes, clears the bake or deletes.
- A new body is named `sim` plus the first mesh's name without its `ArtMesh` prefix (for example `ArtMeshBottomwear` → `simBottomwear`, with a number added on a clash). **Bake all** bakes every enabled body that is unbaked or stale, one after another, each later body on top of the earlier ones' bakes, and commits them as one undo step when all are done; when all are up to date, clicking it again bakes them all again. While a bake runs you can keep editing and also bake other bodies (they queue for the next round); if a body's settings change during its bake, its result is not committed with the old settings but baked again; a body deleted during the bake has its result discarded. **Bake automatically** is a local UI preference, not saved with the project. While baking, the status bar shows the current body, its number and the progress; when a single body fails to bake, it keeps its old bake and a note appears below the list, while the rest commit as usual.
- A simulation reads only what runs before it in the stack; when a card before it changes (a modifier moved before it, say), its bake reports stale.

## Model presets

The **Physics & Simulation** group in the **Model presets** section of the Modifiers panel's **World** page (formerly the Model presets panel) turns common parts into simulations and bakes them in one step. The result is ordinary weight groups and bodies, still adjustable in their simulation cards afterwards. The panel's three groups, **Rigging & Facial**, **Motions** and **Physics & Simulation**, are collapsible rows in the same style as the Parameters panel's folders, with a summary on the right. The **Motions** group only decides whether the model has the two groups **Basic motions** and **Skeleton motions**; single motions are enabled and adjusted in the animation panel. **Physics & Simulation** has one row per item: front hair and back hair each have a dropdown switching between **Simulate / Classic sway / Off**; clothing and eye jelly are checkboxes, where checking applies the preset and unchecking removes it, and the clothing's per-mesh looseness list appears below the clothing row. Grey text beside each control describes its current content (the clothing parts simulated, the part being absent from the artwork; hover for the full text), with a warning colour when unbaked or when the simulation body was deleted. **Selected only (n)** in the top toolbar makes checking act on the selected layers only (unchecking always removes the whole thing), and **Auto pin weights** sits on the right. Bake progress and cancel are in the bottom status bar, and failure reasons appear at the bottom of the group. Each check is one undo step; the MCP counterpart is the `model_apply_preset` tool.

**Generate from PSD** on the **Start** page (leftmost in the workspace tab bar, or **File → Start Page**) gathers the same presets **before** a PSD is imported: **Minimal / Default / Full** at the top of the properties panel mean, respectively, only the rig (no motions, physics or simulation, or mouth outline); both motion groups + classic hair sway + clothing simulation + eye jelly; and the default plus simulated front and back hair and facial feature offsets. Each item can be adjusted below, and the choice is remembered. After that, however the PSD is imported (from the Start page, the **File** menu, recent files or a drop), generation writes the changed presets as one undo step, then generates the hair and clothing simulations in turn (one step each), and switches to the Edit workspace. Clothing is on by default, and when clothing is recognized in the artwork, a clothing simulation is generated and baked. With a model generated from a PSD already open, **Apply to Current Model** in the **Generate** button's menu applies the same presets to it. Splitting layers by mesh no longer pops up with the import; use **Model → Split Layers by Mesh…** instead, which scans every layer and confirms in bulk.

| Preset | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Front hair / Back hair | All meshes of that hair become one hair body (`preset_front_hair` / `preset_back_hair`); each strand (each connected piece of a mesh) is pinned at its own top and freed toward the tip, and only the pin group is written; front hair uses the **Bangs** material, back hair the default hair material. The hair's old sway is removed at the same time (the `ParamHairFront/Back` parameter, the `DeformHair*Physics` deformers and the pendulum), and the meshes (including split-off parts) move under the follow deformer with the artwork staying in place. Choosing **Classic sway** or **Off** deletes the body and restores the old sway (**Off** also disables it) |
| Clothing | Reads the top, bottom, neckwear, hands (sleeves) and legwear layers and simulates only where the artwork looks loose; tight parts follow the rig. Bottoms are first judged as skirt or trousers: by name first (裙, skirt, スカート / 裤, pants, ズボン and so on), otherwise by the alpha silhouette, where a centre gap running from the crotch to the hem means trousers. Torso and bottoms compare their silhouette with the body row by row: the body silhouette starts at the shoulder line (for bottoms, the waistline) and may only widen slowly downward, and whatever sticks out beyond it counts as loose (ruffles, bows, flared hems, open coat fronts); a top below the waistline (the bottoms' waistline, or the top of legwear reaching under the top), a skirt below the hips where it is wider than the hips, and neckwear below the knot count as hanging free, while straight trousers and pencil skirts stay tight. Sleeves and legwear bend with the limbs, so they use a morphological opening instead: only parts thinner than a disc half the limb's width (cuff / sock ruffles, ribbons) count as loose, while puff sleeves and turned-up cuffs follow the limb. Skin-coloured areas always stay tight, so spread fingers are not simulated either. Looseness grows with the in-layer distance from the tight area and produces pin (1 − looseness), mass and wind (rising with looseness, less wind on limbs). Meshes without enough loose vertices get no body and are taken out of the clothing preset body they were in. Bodies are split by part into `preset_top`, `preset_skirt`, `preset_trousers`, `preset_neckwear`, `preset_sleeves` and `preset_legwear`; trousers, sleeves and legwear are stiffer. Below the clothing row each mesh is listed with its part and a looseness bar, tight ones merged into one row; hover a bottom to see how it was judged. Unchecking deletes every clothing preset body |
| Eye jelly | Switches the jelly pendulum of the eyes |
| **Auto pin weights** (toolbar) | Recomputes the weight groups above by part type for the selected layers or all simulated meshes, and bakes again the bodies that use them, without creating bodies |

The groups presets write are named `preset_pin`, `preset_mass` and `preset_wind`; when a body already names its own groups in `groups`, those groups are refreshed in place. Applying the same preset again updates the existing body rather than adding another. Meshes already in a body the user made are refused. The wind group currently takes effect only when MCP `simulation_simulate` is given wind (see above).

## MCP

Simulation is split into several tools by operation. Parameters go in `{"request": {...}}`, every tool picks the body by `id` and carries `project_id`, `state` and `request_id`; they return a background job handle, and `job_wait` / `job_get` fetch the result:

- `simulation_put`: creates or partially changes a body by `id`, with the fields `targets`, `material`, `material_preset` (the preset names above: `cotton`, `silk`, `chiffon`, `wool`, `denim`, `leather`, `elastic`, `hair`, `fine_hair`, `thick_hair`, `bangs`; `material` fields given alongside override its values), `groups`, `glue_roles`, `inputs`, `modes`, `keys`, `exaggeration`, `static_inputs`, `blend_shapes` (`true` / `false` / `null` for auto), `auto_bake`, `output_names` (names by parameter or pendulum ID, replaced as a whole), `input_ranges` (`[min, max]` training ranges by input parameter, replaced as a whole), `vertical` (`true` / `false` / `null` for auto), `outputs` (`{id, range, gain}` by generated mode parameter ID, replaced as a whole, no new bake needed), `force_inputs` (inputs kept even when they barely move the body) and `training_clips` (motions to train on, `{motion: 0.05–1}`, replaced as a whole). A new body without `inputs` gets the default inputs, and an empty array means no inputs. With `auto_bake` on (the default) it bakes again in the same step and returns a `bake` summary or `bake_error`;
- `simulation_simulate`: a read-only trial run (no matching button in the panel); each input is held for `hold` seconds then released and watched for `release` seconds, with optional `wind`; returns peaks, drift at rest, stretch and setting hints;
- `simulation_bake`: bakes (a few seconds) and returns each mode parameter and its swing, the key count, the pendulum ID and segment count, the separate pendulums' IDs (`own_pendulums`, when any), how each input was read (`inputs`: how far it moves the body, whether it only translates and rotates, its group or left out), each group's visual check on the checking motion (`visual`), the bake method (`method`), and on the checking motion `fit_r2`, `error_p95_px`, `parameter_peak` (the largest share of their range the mode parameters use), `clipped_frames` (share of frames at an end) and `jerk_ratio` (jerk over the simulation's);
- `simulation_compare`: a read-only background job that drives the reference simulation and the exported model separately with the motions in `motions` (user motion IDs or built-in names, up to 8) and returns each motion's visual check;
- `simulation_clear_bake`: clears the bake;
- `simulation_delete`: deletes the body.

The reference simulation preview is an audition session that does not enter the document or history: `simulation_preview` starts with `mode=start` and `simulation_id` (`restart` / `stop` take `session_id`), `simulation_preview_step` advances 1–240 steps of `dt` seconds (at most 0.1), `simulation_preview_get` reads the current frame and `simulation_preview_render` renders it.

`vertex_group_update` makes weight groups from rules (`fill`, `outline`, `gradient`, `glue`, `region`), `delete=true` deletes one, and no per-vertex arrays are passed; `vertex_group_paint` changes weights with the same brush, gradient and invert as the canvas. `workspace_inspect` with `scope=simulations` lists bodies, glue keys and bake summaries (including `bake_stale`), and `scope=vertex_groups` lists weight groups.

## Current scope

- Implemented: cloth and hair (XPBD solver, mass by area, triangle area and anti-flip, rotation-based bending, automatic grain and anisotropy, pins, glue roles, long-range constraints), vertex groups, the reference simulation preview, baking into parameters + keyforms (blend shapes or keyform axes, chosen automatically) + pendulums and exporting them, automatic baking, exaggeration; model presets (hair simulation replacing the old sway, simulating top / skirt / trousers / neckwear / sleeves / legwear by looseness, auto pin weights).
- Tested: the tml back hair comparison data in the table above. Model preset bakes on tml (checking-motion R² / 95% error, measured on the old solver, to be measured again under the new material model): back hair 0.69 / 229 px, the same as pinning the top 10% by hand; front hair 0.25 / 185 px, 0.2–0.3 with any pinned range. The front hair's problem is the material, not the pinned range: bangs sweeping across the forehead do not carry gravity along the strand length the way back hair does, and the default hair material's weak spring back turns them into a soft spring of about 0.75 Hz, straying 74 px from the skeleton when shaking the head (1.2 Hz; the skeleton moves them 33 px) and 47 px when nodding. With the **Bangs** material (new material model, tml / ds): 41 / 30 px shaking the head, 12 / 10 px nodding, 10 / 6 px left after a head turn stops, bake R² 0.18 → 0.27 / 0.21 → 0.49, 95% error 216 → 85 / 112 → 57 px. The skirt data was measured with the collision feature that has since been removed and needs testing again. Adding mass / wind groups to hair lowers the back hair's R² from 0.69 to 0.56, so the hair presets do not write those two groups.
- Obstacles: a body can carry obstacles (`obstacles`), circles or capsules following another mesh that keep particles outside; they take part in the reference simulation and baking, and export to runtime targets that play the live simulation (p2lrt, web). For now they can only be authored through MCP `simulation_put`; there is no editing on the canvas yet. The older mesh colliders (by margin) proved unreliable in testing and were removed; such colliders and collision weight groups in old projects are ignored on load.
- Not implemented yet: exporting wind and other force fields, rigid and soft bodies, baking into ordinary keys (dropping the generator).
- Looseness heat map checked by eye (tml, ds and one chibi set): on tml only the lower half of the skirt is loose; on ds the apron, the bow at the back of the waist and the hem are loose, while the puff sleeves, cuffs and sock tops are tight; on the chibi set the open coat is loose below the waistline and the straight trousers are tight.
- To be tested: how the skirt and hair of the example models look in the official runtime after export; how natural the hair and clothing simulations made by the model presets are.
