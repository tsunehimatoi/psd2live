# Simulation and baking

[Documentation](../../README.md) · [中文](../../zh/guide/SIMULATION.md) · [Physics](PHYSICS.md) · [Swing generation](SWING.md) · [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md) · [MCP usage and interface](../agent/MCP_AUTHORING.md)

For a first run, start with the [illustrated simulation tutorial](SIMULATION_TUTORIAL.md): it walks through the presets, pin weights, baking again and checking the result with screenshots.

Simulation runs a real 2D cloth / hair simulation on meshes in the editor: vertices are held by pins and driven by gravity and inertia. The Cubism runtime does no such simulation, so **the simulation itself is not exported**. To export it, it has to be **baked** into content Cubism can play: mesh keyforms on new parameters, driven by a fitted pendulum, plus exact corrections on a few parameters. The bake is stored in the project; rebuilding the model writes back only the result and does not simulate again.

## Basic workflow

1. Select the skirt or hair meshes on the canvas, then in the **Simulation** panel (after the **Physics** tab in every workspace, shown by default in the Edit workspace) click **New** and choose **Cloth** or **Hair**. Several meshes in one body simulate together.
2. Set the pins. Use either way, or both:
   - in the panel's **Vertex groups**, click **Paint** on the **Pin** row, or switch the canvas to **Simulate** mode, and paint the **Pin** group with the weight brush;
   - in the panel's **Glue**, set the glue joining the waist to **Pin**.
3. Turn the toolbar's preview switch on for **Reference sim**, turn the head and shake the body in the preview, and adjust the material until it looks natural.
4. Click **Bake** in the selected body's **Bake** section (the first section of the editor), or **Bake all** in the panel's top toolbar. A bake takes a few seconds and runs in the background; you can keep editing other things meanwhile, and it is committed as a separate undo step on whatever the latest version is then. The toolbar's **Bake automatically** switch is off by default; when on, every change in the panel commits at once and then bakes again in the background. After a bake, the Physics panel shows a pendulum group whose origin is **Simulation**, the Parameters panel shows `ParamSim…` parameters, and exports include them.
5. Turn the preview switch off to go back to **Export**: the preview now plays the exported model's motion, to compare with the reference simulation.

### Panel layout

The panel has the same layout as the Physics panel:

- **Toolbar**: one row holds **New** (Cloth / Hair), **Bake all**, the **Bake automatically** switch and the preview switch (off is **Export**, on is **Reference sim**; the label shows the current one); the row below shows the bake state. On the right are expand all and collapse all (the body list and the editor sections), and with the reference simulation on, a reset button at the far right puts the live simulation back at rest. **New** creates a body from the selected meshes, named `sim` plus the first mesh's name without its `ArtMesh` prefix (for example `ArtMeshBottomwear` → `simBottomwear`, with a number added on a clash). **Bake all** bakes every enabled body that is unbaked or stale, one after another, each later body on top of the earlier ones' bakes, and commits them as one undo step when all are done; when all are up to date, clicking it again bakes them all again. While a bake runs you can keep editing and also bake other bodies (they queue for the next round); if a body's settings change during its bake, its result is not committed with the old settings but baked again; a body deleted during the bake has its result discarded. **Bake automatically** is a local UI preference, not saved with the project, and off by default. Beside the buttons a count shows the state: "1/2 need baking" in yellow when something waits, otherwise "2/2 baked". While baking, the button turns into Cancel (cancelling commits nothing), and the state shows the current body, its number and the progress; when a single body fails to bake, it keeps its old bake and a note appears below the list, while the rest commit as usual.
- **List**: a collapsible **Bodies** folder. The checkbox at the left of each row enables / disables the body; on the right are the bake state and kind: baked shows only a green dot, and only states that need attention are written out (yellow **Stale**, meaning export still uses the old result; red **Not baked**, meaning it does not export; grey **Off**). The context menu bakes, clears the bake or deletes.
- **Editor**: the title row holds the name and kind (switching kind applies that kind's material preset), followed by **Bake**, **Material**, **Inputs**, **Outputs**, **Glue** and **Vertex groups**. The Bake section first shows the visual check on unseen motion (swing ratio, lag, settling, jitter, in warning colour when the difference shows), then R² and the error (with separate warnings for hitting the ends or extra jitter), then **Bake / Bake again**, **Clear bake**, Modes, Up/down, Keys and Exaggeration; Modes as, Train on motions, Exact poses and each mode's swing sit under **Advanced**. The **Compare** section (collapsed by default) puts the bake beside the reference simulation; see [Export and reference simulation](#export-and-reference-simulation). The Material section offers named presets at its top, and once the material changes, a **Back to the preset of this kind** button appears in the section title. The Inputs section lists each parameter's name and ID, marking parameters no longer in the model as **missing** in warning colour; after a bake, each input shows below it how far it moves the body, and left-out ones are marked **Left out** in warning colour with a **Keep** checkbox; the row below that is its training range. When the inputs differ from the default, a **Reset to the default inputs** button appears in the section title, and with no inputs the section warns that it cannot bake. After a bake, the Outputs section lists the mode parameters and pendulums made (name and ID); click a name to rename it, and clearing it or setting the default name back returns to naming after the body. Each mode parameter takes an ID, range and gain below it.

## Vertex groups

A vertex group is a 0–1 weight per vertex on each mesh. Groups exist only in this application and are not written into `.moc3` / `.cmo3`. Each mesh has one group per kind, told apart by kind, with no name needed. They are painted in **Simulate** mode with the weight brush or the weight gradient (see [Canvas editing](CANVAS_EDITOR.md)): the left toolbar lists the kinds below the weight tools (`1`–`5` switch too), and clicking one switches the group being painted; the weight brush offers circle, line and rectangle tips as well. After topology edits, weights are kept by interpolating from each vertex's origin.

| Kind | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Pin (`1`) | Vertices held by the rig; the weight is how firmly |
| Stiffness (`2`) | Scales stretch and bend stiffness per vertex, for a stiff root and a soft hem |
| Shape (`3`) | Scales the spring back toward the drawn shape per vertex |
| Mass (`4`) | Scales mass per vertex |
| Damping (`5`) | Scales damping per vertex |

The panel's **Vertex groups** lists whether each kind's group has been painted on the target meshes. **Paint** on each row selects the first target mesh, switches the canvas to **Simulate** mode and opens the weight brush for that kind. Wind and other force fields are not implemented yet, so the panel and the weight brush offer no **Wind** group; MCP's `vertex_group_update` / `vertex_group_paint` still accept `wind`, which takes effect only when `simulation_simulate` is given `wind`. MCP can still use named groups and pick them by name in the body's `groups`; with several groups of one kind, the first one is used unless one is named, which is the one the UI paints.

## Glue

Glue never becomes a pin on its own; each glue joined to a simulated mesh is chosen separately in the panel:

| Role | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Ignore (default) | The simulation leaves it alone; it only welds at runtime |
| Pin | The glued vertices on the simulated side follow the other mesh's actual deformation, for example a skirt and the waist |
| Hold together | Keeps the seam joined when both sides simulate, for example separate front and back panels |

## Material

| Item | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Mass | Relative density. Each vertex's mass comes from its share of the mesh area (a third of the area of the triangles around it), so refining or re-triangulating a mesh only adds deformation detail and does not make the cloth heavier or softer |
| Stretch | Resistance to getting longer; cloth and hair should be near 1 |
| Bend | How sharply it may bend: the difference in angle between neighbouring triangles. It governs bending only, not shrinking |
| Area | How firmly each triangle keeps its area. Seen from the front, cloth shortens and bunches up when it folds in depth; low values let light cloth shrink this way, high values keep heavy parts in shape. Whatever the value, triangles never flip or collapse to zero area |
| Grain | How much softer it is across the grain than along it. The grain direction is taken automatically as "away from the pins" (the direction in which path length measured along the mesh grows), or straight down when there are no pins. At high values strands do not stretch lengthwise but can part sideways and fold lengthwise |
| Damping | How fast motion dies down (per second) |
| Shape | A spring back toward the drawn shape; 0 is pure physics. It is per unit mass, so mesh density does not change how fast it springs back |
| Slack | How far beyond its resting path a vertex's distance from the pins may grow in a hard fling |

Defaults: cloth Area 0.3, Grain 0.3; hair Area 0.5, Grain 0.7. The sleeve, trousers and legwear presets use Area 0.5, neckwear 0.4.

The **Preset** dropdown fills the items above with the values of a named material, which you can keep adjusting afterwards; the project saves only the values, and the dropdown shows a preset's name when the values match it, otherwise **Custom**.

| Kind | Preset | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloth | Cotton (default) | The defaults in the table above |
| Cloth | Silk | Light and soft, with fine folds; swings long and stops slowly |
| Cloth | Chiffon | Lightest and softest; stopped by air damping sooner than silk |
| Cloth | Wool | Heavy and soft; starts swinging late and stops soon |
| Cloth | Denim | Heavy and stiff; wide folds that keep their shape |
| Cloth | Leather | Stiffest; swings as a whole, hardly bunches, springs back to the drawn shape |
| Cloth | Elastic | Stretches and springs back; for straps and socks |
| Hair | Hair (default) | The defaults in the table above |
| Hair | Fine hair | Light; bends and parts easily, swings long |
| Hair | Thick hair | Heavier; swings as a whole and keeps its shape |
| Hair | Bangs | Lies against the forehead instead of hanging down; stronger spring back and more damping, moves with the head with the tips lagging slightly; used by the front hair preset |

Before the simulation starts it is calibrated so that the drawn shape is the resting equilibrium, so a model at rest does not sag once simulation is on.

The solver is 2D XPBD (16 substeps per frame). Where the rig lengthens something (for example parallax as the head turns), its rest length grows with it instead of fighting the rig's shape; where the rig shortens something, that is taken as turning into depth and is absorbed by soft compression.

## Baking

**Inputs** are the parameters that shake the body, used exactly as listed, with no implicit defaults. A new body (from the panel, from MCP without `inputs`, or from a model preset) gets the default inputs: the head and body angles (`ParamAngleX`, `ParamAngleZ`, `ParamBodyAngleX`, `ParamBodyAngleZ`, where the model has them), plus nodding, body up-down and lean (`ParamAngleY`, `ParamBodyAngleY`, `ParamBodyLean`, where present). For the cloth kind the vertical inputs are only `ParamBodyAngleY`: clothes hang on the body, so nodding does not move them; leaning mostly makes them settle in another pose, a static offset that a vertical pendulum cannot express, so it would be fitted as a pendulum with almost no restoring force, amplified by a huge output scale. When a clothing preset is applied again, bodies still on the old generic default inputs switch to the cloth defaults. An empty list means no inputs, and baking reports an error. Bodies in older projects that recorded no inputs (which then meant the defaults) open showing the full default list above; parameters the model lacks are removed the next time that body is changed. The default inputs and the old "empty" count as the same, so existing bakes do not go stale because of it.

A bake first reads from the rig how each input moves the body: an input that moves no vertex by 1 px or more over its training range is left out of the bake (marked **Left out** in the panel; checking **Keep** or MCP `force_inputs` forces it in), an input that moves the body more up and down than sideways goes to the vertical parameter (see below), and the rest go to the sideways modes. So parameters in the default inputs that have nothing to do with the part (head turns on a skirt, for example) are no longer fitted into the pendulum, and the body does not wobble for no reason when the user moves them.

Each input can have a training range (low to high end, in parameter units): the bake drives it only within that range, which defaults to the parameter's whole range; the range always includes the default value, and any part beyond the parameter's range is clipped. The mode parameters are sized to the hardest motion in training (see step 4), so the narrower the range, the more fully the body swings within it, and the sooner it reaches the ends past it. For example, if `ParamBodyAngleX` usually moves only within ±5, a training range of ±5 lets ordinary motion use the whole mode parameter. Changing a training range makes the bake stale; removing an input removes its training range and Keep setting with it.

**Train on motions** (under **Advanced**, MCP `training_clips`) has the bake learn the model's own motions (user motions or the built-in Idle, Nod, Shake and so on) on top of the designed excitation, each weighted ×0.05–×1. It suits models driven mainly by a few fixed motions rather than face tracking. Each motion counts only toward the group of parameters it moves most. Bake again after editing these motions (their content is not part of the staleness check).

**Outputs** are the mode parameters and pendulums a bake writes. Their names follow the body by default (parameters "<name>", "<name> 2", "<name> Y" and so on, the pendulum named like the body); renames are stored on the body and applied on rebuild, with no new bake needed and the IDs unchanged. Each mode parameter can also take its own exported ID, range and gain, again without a new bake: the ID defaults to the generated `ParamSim<id>_<k>` and can be changed to another ID to reuse motions and physics made for that ID, but it may not clash with another parameter in the model or with this body's inputs; the range is ±1…±100, ±30 by default, and the keys and the pendulum's output scale scale with it, so the motion stays the same; the gain is ×0…×3, the exaggeration by default, and 0 holds it still. The bake itself is always stored with the generated IDs and ±30, and these settings apply on rebuild. Renaming a **Simulation**-origin pendulum in the Physics panel is written here too, and the pendulum is still driven by the bake.

### Settings

| Item | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Modes (1–3, default 2) | How many parameters are made, each driven by one vertex of the fitted pendulum. The first mode is the overall swing; each later mode is one more segment hung below, or a separate pendulum, and only adds what the modes above cannot show: the body bending, bunching up and compressing as it lags. With 1 mode the shape can only move as a whole with the swing angle and cloth lacks compression, so the default is 2; long strands and ribbons can use 3 |
| Up/down (Auto / On / Off) | Whether the vertical inputs bake separately into `ParamSim<id>_Y` (see below). Auto makes it only when they move the body 2 px or more; On always makes it; Off leaves the vertical inputs out of the bake, and an error is reported when there are only vertical inputs |
| Keys (3 / 5 / 7 / 9) | Keys on each mode parameter and each exact-pose parameter, evenly spread. More keys follow arcs more closely; they add keyforms, not parameters |
| Exaggeration (×1–×2, default ×1.3) | How much larger than the simulation the modes swing. It applies when the keyforms are written back, so a change takes effect at once without a new bake, and it never pushes the parameters to their ends. Anime style usually looks better a little larger than life |
| Exact poses | Parameters baked as exact corrections on their own axes (up to four); none by default |
| Modes as | Auto (default), Blend shapes or Keyform axes. Both play the same. Keyform axes are read by every Cubism version, but each mode multiplies the mesh's keyform count by its key count; blend shapes (Cubism 4.2+) add their keyforms on top of the existing ones. Auto uses blend shapes only when some target mesh would exceed 64 keyforms; when the target version has no blend shapes, keyform axes are always written |

Blend shapes save keyforms, not parameters: for example, a mesh with 9 keyforms and 2 modes of 5 keys each gets 9 × 5 × 5 = 225 keyforms as keyform axes, or 9 keyforms plus 8 blend shapes as blend shapes.

### Steps

1. **Exact poses**: the rig is posed at every key of each exact-pose parameter, the body is left to settle, and its difference from the rig is written as correction keyforms on that parameter's axis. Skipped when no exact-pose parameter is set.
2. **Driving each input on its own**: every input in the bake is driven alone while the others stay at their defaults: one segment turns it to the top of its training range within 0.15 s, holds until the body settles (up to 1.6 s), returns to the default, turns to the bottom and back; another is a logarithmic sweep from 0.25 Hz to 3 Hz, with the amplitude tapering near 3 Hz. Each segment starts at rest and is simulated in parallel on its own copy of the body; the sideways and vertical groups share these simulations. Every frame records the difference between the body and the rig (plus the exact-pose corrections), converted into each mesh's own keyform coordinates. Each group also has a motion in which its inputs are dragged, shaken and idly swayed together, used only for checking. The segments are weighted to balance their amount of motion (by up to 100×), so inputs that move the body little are also fitted correctly instead of being drowned out by big flings.
   Previously all inputs moved randomly together in one motion, and the fit could not tell which input moved the body: on the `tml` back hair, `ParamBodyAngleX` moves the hair roots about 70 px, yet the pendulum gave it only 1% weight, and when following a mouse drag the exported swing was only about a fifth of the simulation's. Driven one at a time, each input gets a weight that makes the body move the way it does.
3. **Pendulum**: one Cubism pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>` is fitted, with as many segments as modes, mode by mode: first only the first mode's segment, then each later mode in turn, each fitting only the remaining motion the modes above cannot read, in that remaining motion's own principal direction (not the overall motion's), and finally all segments and weights are refined together for one round; readout always solves the modes one by one in order. A later mode has two kinds of candidates: one more segment hung below the pendulum (a pendulum's vertex is affected only by the vertices above it, so adding a segment does not change the modes above; searched from 4 starting points), or a separate single-segment pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>_<k>` with its own inputs, length and reaction speed (compression may react to acceleration faster and harder than the swing). Whichever does better on the checking motion is used; the parameter count stays the same. Fitted all together, neighbouring vertices swing almost in sync, and the two modes' shapes grow large and cancel each other, distorting motion outside training. The score looks at the overall effect, not just the error: how closely it follows the simulation, how much it flings out and swings back after the inputs stop (counted once more on its own), and whether any segment of motion is more jittery than the simulation. Each input's type (X translation or angle) and initial weight are estimated by least squares on small superposed motions (with up to 6 inputs, every combination of types is compared and the one with the least residual error taken); Nelder–Mead then searches the segments' lengths, reaction speeds, sway, convergence speed and the inputs' weights together; every candidate is scored by playing all the training motions once at their real amplitude with the same computation as the export. Of the candidates, the one scoring best on the checking motion wins.
4. **Mode parameters**: the angle of the pendulum's k-th vertex is written into the -30…30 parameter `ParamSim<id>_<k>`. The output scale is set from the pendulum's largest angle over all training and checking motions plus a 10% margin, so the parameter does not reach ±30: while a parameter sits at its end the hair stops, which reads as a stutter. The range is ±30 rather than ±1 because keyform axes snap values within 0.001 of a key onto the key (so does the Cubism runtime); in a ±1 range, a large body would hitch on that snap every time it swings past rest.
5. **Keyforms**: solved mode by mode by least squares: each mode's keyforms fit the shape left after the modes before it, and each frame's shape is the sum of the keyforms interpolated at each parameter's value as the pendulum plays. The keyforms are constrained to a smooth curve with no sharp turn at neighbouring keys and a straight path through the rest key; otherwise the speed jumps as the parameter sweeps past a key, which is another hitch. Then two adjustments are tried on the checking motion and kept only when they follow better without more jitter: each mode's keyforms are solved again with the other modes subtracted (closer to solving together, without cancelling out); and later modes try gains of ×0.25, ×0.5 and ×0.75, so they do not add noise to small motions. Parameters that hardly move are not made.

**Vertical motion baked separately**: a Cubism pendulum hangs along gravity and outputs only an angle; when its root moves straight up and down the angle does not change, so no choice of inputs can make it express vertical motion. The approach matches an artist's `裙y` and `长发y` physics groups: inputs that move the body more up and down than sideways (usually nodding, body up-down and lean) are separated from the others and run through steps 3–5 on their own: a single-segment pendulum `PhysicsSim_<id>_y` is fitted to drive one mode parameter `ParamSim<id>_Y` (named "<simulation name> Y"), whose keyforms are the body's vertical lag and bounce. The two groups' keyforms add up on the mesh. When vertical motion is under 2 px, this parameter is not made (with Up/down On it is always made; with Off it is not made and the vertical inputs stay out of the bake). With only vertical inputs, every mode is fitted this way. The `tml` skirt: squatting / rising leaves residuals of up to 47 px in the simulation, and the old bake kept only a static tilt; baked separately, R² on the checking motion rises from 0.37 to 0.61.

R² is not the only measure of looking right: when the swing is a little small or the phase slightly off, least squares shrinks the keyforms as a whole to buy a lower error, and the exported motion looks lifeless. So after a bake the panel first shows each group's visual check on the checking motion:

| Metric | Meaning | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swing | The tip's swing (the tenth of vertices that move most) along the simulation's principal direction, export over simulation | Outside ×0.75–×1.3 |
| Lag | How many milliseconds the export trails the simulation (cross-correlation peak) | Over 80 ms |
| Settling | Time to come back to rest after the inputs stop, export over simulation | Outside ×0.6–×1.6 |
| Jitter | Square root of the ratio of third-difference energies | Over ×1.5 |

An opposite direction warns too. MCP also returns direction agreement, the difference in swing-back count, the share of frames at an end or stuck, how much strands shorten and the offset at rest. The next row is R² and the largest deviation from the simulation over 95% of frames; when frames reach an end or jerk exceeds 1.5× the simulation's, a separate row warns. Each mode parameter's swing is under **Advanced**. MCP `simulation_bake` returns every metric and how each input was read (`inputs`).

Baked pendulum groups, like ordinary generated groups, can be viewed, changed or turned off in the Physics panel. Changes in the Physics panel are overrides on the generated pendulum; after baking again or changing an output's ID or range, the overrides carry over to the new pendulum by three-way merge: only the fields you changed are kept (output scale kept as a ratio to the original value), the rest take the newly fitted values, outputs follow parameter renames, and overrides that end up equal to the new pendulum are removed. When clearing the bake, deleting the body or a new bake no longer makes some pendulum, its overrides, disabled state and order are removed with it.

### Results and timing

Bodies made by the model presets (2 modes, 5 keys, exaggeration ×1) were compared frame by frame between the reference simulation and the exported model (Cubism pendulum + keyforms) on 13–15 motions not seen during baking: face-tracking-like mouse following, head and body tilting sideways, the built-in Idle / Nod / Shake, a step on each input, head turns, dragging and shaking. Only motions in which the tip moves 10 px or more in the simulation are counted (segment counts in parentheses): the other motions are driven only by left-out inputs, with only a slight wobble as the body starts in the simulation, and counting them would pull every metric toward 0. "Right direction" counts motions whose swing direction agrees (cosine ≥ 0.9); swing is the factor converted back from the median of how far each motion's export-to-simulation ratio is from 1; R² is the mean over motions (values below -1 count as -1); rest offset is the distance between export and simulation while the inputs have been held for 1 s or more (95th percentile). The first value is the old result trained on a motion library, the second is driving each input on its own.

- Hair improves most, especially on motions close to real use: on the tml back hair following the mouse, the swing goes from 0.19× to 1.04× the simulation's and R² from -0.15 to 0.92; R² on the Shake motion goes from 0.07 to 0.83, and the `ParamBodyAngleX` step from not moving at all to R² 0.86.
- Driving each input on its own bakes cloth 2–4× faster, but R² is lower than before (about 0.3 for the skirt and top, previously 0.35–0.5). The vertical parameter is still weak (`ParamBodyAngleY` step R² 0.17–0.30, swing 0.2–0.8×): in the simulation the hem flings out to the same side whether the body moves up or down, and swings back on one side only, while the pendulum's output is symmetric in the input's sign, so the fit can only compromise.
- The back hair's swing period for the body tilting sideways (`ParamBodyAngleZ`, about 1.3 s) differs from that for head turns (about 0.9 s), and so does its shape; with 2 modes it gets no mode of its own (the ds back hair export hardly moves; the tml back hair swings 0.59× with R² 0.11); 3 modes do not help either.
- Inputs that barely move cloth, such as head turns (about 2 px in the simulation), are left out, so the skirt does not wobble for no reason when the user turns the head.

Timings were measured on a 32-thread desktop; simulation and fitting run in parallel across cores, so they are slower with fewer cores. The table is produced by the development tool `SimBakeSuite`, which outputs every visual metric and a tip trajectory plot for each motion; see [Development tools](DEVELOPMENT.md#development-tools) for usage.

### Staleness

A bake records the settings, target meshes, weight groups, related glue and solver version it was based on (after a solver change, bakes in old projects show as stale). When any of them change, the panel notes that the bake is stale. With the toolbar's automatic baking on, panel changes are committed and then baked again in the background (as a separate undo step), with the search starting from the last pendulum, which is faster than baking from scratch; applying a model preset likewise commits first and then bakes the bodies it made in the background; MCP `simulation_put` follows the body's own `auto_bake` (on by default); changes made elsewhere (meshes, weights) still show as stale. A stale bake still exports; click **Bake again** to update it, and **Clear bake** removes its parameters, keyforms and pendulum. When automatic baking fails or is cancelled, the old bake is kept and the reason shown. When a target mesh is rebuilt (its vertex count changes), that mesh's bake result is skipped with a note in the panel.

### Export and reference simulation

The toolbars at the top of the panel and in the canvas's **Simulate** mode switch what the preview shows:

- **Export**: the preview plays the bake, that is, the parameters and keyforms driven by the fitted Cubism pendulum, matching the exported model's motion. Unbaked bodies do not move here, and the bottom-left corner of the canvas notes "Unbaked simulation".
- **Reference sim**: the canvas shows the editor's own physics simulation, for comparison only, never exported. The bottom-left corner of the canvas marks "Reference simulation · not exported" in warning colour, and the body's own `ParamSim…` parameters stay still meanwhile, so nothing is applied twice.

The body editor's **Compare** section puts the two side by side:

- **View** (available while the reference simulation is on): how the first preview canvas shows the reference simulation: **Reference** (in place of the export, as above), **Export** (the export alone) or **Overlay** (the reference simulation drawn semi-transparent over the export, only the simulated layers). Each canvas remembers its own, saved with the workspace.
- **Side by side**: turns the reference simulation on and makes sure there are two preview canvases, the first showing the export and the second the reference, both following the same pose and playing together.
- **Play**: pick a motion from the dropdown (the built-in Idle, Nod and Shake, skeleton motions when there are bones, and user motions); the reference simulation and the exported pendulum both return to rest, then play it, for comparing segment by segment.
- **Check**: drives the reference simulation and the exported model separately with the chosen motion in the background (gain ×1) and gives the same visual check as the Bake section; the MCP counterpart is `simulation_compare`.

## Model presets

The **Physics & Simulation** group of the **Model presets** panel (formerly Model Settings) turns common parts into simulations and bakes them in one step. The result is ordinary weight groups and bodies, still adjustable in the **Simulation** panel afterwards. The panel's three groups, **Rigging & Facial**, **Motions** and **Physics & Simulation**, are collapsible rows in the same style as the Parameters panel's folders, with a summary on the right. The **Motions** group only decides whether the model has the two groups **Basic motions** and **Skeleton motions**; single motions are enabled and adjusted in the animation panel. **Physics & Simulation** has one row per item: front hair and back hair each have a dropdown switching between **Simulate / Classic sway / Off**; clothing and eye jelly are checkboxes, where checking applies the preset and unchecking removes it, and the clothing's per-mesh looseness list appears below the clothing row. Grey text beside each control describes its current content (the clothing parts simulated, the part being absent from the artwork; hover for the full text), with a warning colour when unbaked or when the simulation body was deleted. **Selected only (n)** in the top toolbar makes checking act on the selected layers only (unchecking always removes the whole thing), and **Auto pin weights** sits on the right. Bake progress and cancel are in the bottom status bar, and failure reasons appear at the bottom of the group. Each check is one undo step; the MCP counterpart is the `model_apply_preset` tool.

The same panel's **Rig modules** group lists the modules of the generated rig by area (head and face, eyes, brows, mouth and lower face, hair, body, clothing and accessories, expressions, motions). A card covers both sides; each module has a row with its switch and, in grey, what it adds for the current art - parameters, keyforms and physics groups (estimated, marked "≈", while off) - and, when on, its few adjustable values. Only modules that apply to the art are listed. The preset at the top (Full / Standard / Minimal) switches them by importance, the parameter budget switches off the least important and costliest until the modules add no more parameters than it says, and the button beside resets them. New projects have every module on; a project saved before modules generates as it did, and the group offers its upgrade. Every change is one undo step; over MCP, `module_catalog` lists them and `settings_update` `changes.rig` changes them (see the [rig preset architecture](../../zh/spec/RIG_PRESETS.md), Chinese).

**Generate from PSD** on the **Start** page (leftmost in the workspace tab bar, or **File → Start Page**) gathers the same presets **before** a PSD is imported: **Minimal / Default / Full** at the top of the properties panel mean, respectively, only the rig (no motions, physics or simulation, or mouth outline); both motion groups + classic hair sway + clothing simulation + eye jelly; and the default plus simulated front and back hair and facial feature offsets. Each item can be adjusted below, and the choice is remembered. After that, however the PSD is imported (from the Start page, the **File** menu, recent files or a drop), generation writes the changed presets as one undo step, then generates the hair and clothing simulations in turn (one step each), and switches to the Edit workspace. Clothing is on by default, and when clothing is recognized in the artwork, a clothing simulation is generated and baked. With a model generated from a PSD already open, **Apply to Current Model** in the **Generate** button's menu applies the same presets to it. Splitting layers by mesh no longer pops up with the import; use **Model → Split Layers by Mesh…** instead, which scans every layer and confirms in bulk.

| Preset | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Front hair / Back hair | All meshes of that hair become one hair body (`preset_front_hair` / `preset_back_hair`); each strand (each connected piece of a mesh) is pinned at its own top and freed toward the tip, and only the pin group is written; front hair uses the **Bangs** material, back hair the default hair material. The hair's old sway is removed at the same time (the `ParamHairFront/Back` parameter, the `DeformHair*Physics` deformers and the pendulum), and the meshes (including split-off parts) move under the follow deformer with the artwork staying in place. Choosing **Classic sway** or **Off** deletes the body and restores the old sway (**Off** also disables it) |
| Clothing | Reads the top, bottom, neckwear, hands (sleeves) and legwear layers and simulates only where the artwork looks loose; tight parts follow the rig. Bottoms are first judged as skirt or trousers: by name first (裙, skirt, スカート / 裤, pants, ズボン and so on), otherwise by the alpha silhouette, where a centre gap running from the crotch to the hem means trousers. Torso and bottoms compare their silhouette with the body row by row: the body silhouette starts at the shoulder line (for bottoms, the waistline) and may only widen slowly downward, and whatever sticks out beyond it counts as loose (ruffles, bows, flared hems, open coat fronts); a top below the waistline (the bottoms' waistline, or the top of legwear reaching under the top), a skirt below the hips where it is wider than the hips, and neckwear below the knot count as hanging free, while straight trousers and pencil skirts stay tight. Sleeves and legwear bend with the limbs, so they use a morphological opening instead: only parts thinner than a disc half the limb's width (cuff / sock ruffles, ribbons) count as loose, while puff sleeves and turned-up cuffs follow the limb. Skin-coloured areas always stay tight, so spread fingers are not simulated either. Looseness grows with the in-layer distance from the tight area and produces pin (1 − looseness), mass and wind (rising with looseness, less wind on limbs). Meshes without enough loose vertices get no body and are taken out of the clothing preset body they were in. Bodies are split by part into `preset_top`, `preset_skirt`, `preset_trousers`, `preset_neckwear`, `preset_sleeves` and `preset_legwear`; trousers, sleeves and legwear are stiffer. Below the clothing row each mesh is listed with its part and a looseness bar, tight ones merged into one row; hover a bottom to see how it was judged. Unchecking deletes every clothing preset body |
| Eye jelly | Switches the jelly pendulum of the eyes |
| **Auto pin weights** (toolbar) | Recomputes the weight groups above by part type for the selected layers or all simulated meshes, and bakes again the bodies that use them, without creating bodies |

The groups presets write are named `preset_pin`, `preset_mass` and `preset_wind`; when a body already names its own groups in `groups`, those groups are refreshed in place. Applying the same preset again updates the existing body rather than adding another. Meshes already in a body the user made are refused. The wind group currently takes effect only when MCP `simulation_simulate` is given wind (see above).

## MCP

Simulation is split into several tools by operation. Parameters go in `{"request": {...}}`, every tool picks the body by `id` and carries `project_id`, `state` and `request_id`; they return a background job handle, and `job_wait` / `job_get` fetch the result:

- `simulation_put`: creates or partially changes a body by `id`, with the fields `targets`, `material`, `material_preset` (the preset names above: `cotton`, `silk`, `chiffon`, `wool`, `denim`, `leather`, `elastic`, `hair`, `fine_hair`, `thick_hair`, `bangs`; `material` fields given alongside override its values), `groups`, `glue_roles`, `inputs`, `modes`, `keys`, `exaggeration`, `static_inputs`, `blend_shapes` (`true` / `false` / `null` for auto), `auto_bake`, `output_names` (names by parameter or pendulum ID, replaced as a whole), `input_ranges` (`[min, max]` training ranges by input parameter, replaced as a whole), `vertical` (`true` / `false` / `null` for auto), `outputs` (`{id, range, gain}` by generated mode parameter ID, replaced as a whole, no new bake needed), `force_inputs` (inputs kept even when they barely move the body) and `training_clips` (motions to train on, `{motion: 0.05–1}`, replaced as a whole). A new body without `inputs` gets the default inputs, and an empty array means no inputs. With `auto_bake` on (the default) it bakes again in the same step and returns a `bake` summary or `bake_error`;
- `simulation_simulate`: a read-only trial run (no matching button in the panel); each input is held for `hold` seconds then released and watched for `release` seconds, with optional `wind`; returns peaks, drift at rest, stretch and setting hints;
- `simulation_bake`: bakes (a few seconds) and returns each mode parameter and its swing, the key count, the pendulum ID and segment count, the separate pendulums' IDs (`own_pendulums`, when any), how each input was read (`inputs`: how far it moves the body, whether it only translates and rotates, its group or left out), each group's visual check on the checking motion (`visual`), the bake method (`method`), and on the checking motion `fit_r2`, `error_p95_px`, `parameter_peak` (the largest share of their range the mode parameters use), `clipped_frames` (share of frames at an end) and `jerk_ratio` (jerk over the simulation's);
- `simulation_compare`: a read-only background job that drives the reference simulation and the exported model separately with the motions in `motions` (user motion IDs or built-in names, up to 8) and returns each motion's visual check;
- `simulation_clear_bake`: clears the bake;
- `simulation_delete`: deletes the body.

The reference simulation preview is an audition session that does not enter the document or history: `simulation_preview` starts with `mode=start` and `simulation_id` (`restart` / `stop` take `session_id`), `simulation_preview_step` advances 1–240 steps of `dt` seconds (at most 0.1), `simulation_preview_get` reads the current frame and `simulation_preview_render` renders it.

`vertex_group_update` makes weight groups from rules (`fill`, `outline`, `gradient`, `glue`, `region`), `delete=true` deletes one, and no per-vertex arrays are passed; `vertex_group_paint` changes weights with the same brush, gradient and invert as the canvas. `workspace_inspect` with `scope=simulations` lists bodies, glue keys and bake summaries (including `bake_stale`), and `scope=vertex_groups` lists weight groups.

## Current scope

- Implemented: cloth and hair (XPBD solver, mass by area, triangle area and anti-flip, rotation-based bending, automatic grain and anisotropy, pins, glue roles, long-range constraints), vertex groups, the reference simulation preview, baking into parameters + keyforms (blend shapes or keyform axes, chosen automatically) + pendulums and exporting them, automatic baking, exaggeration; model presets (hair simulation replacing the old sway, simulating top / skirt / trousers / neckwear / sleeves / legwear by looseness, auto pin weights).
- Tested: the tml back hair comparison data in the table above. Model preset bakes on tml (checking-motion R² / 95% error, measured on the old solver, to be measured again under the new material model): back hair 0.69 / 229 px, the same as pinning the top 10% by hand; front hair 0.25 / 185 px, 0.2–0.3 with any pinned range. The front hair's problem is the material, not the pinned range: bangs sweeping across the forehead do not carry gravity along the strand length the way back hair does, and the default hair material's weak spring back turns them into a soft spring of about 0.75 Hz, straying 74 px from the skeleton when shaking the head (1.2 Hz; the skeleton moves them 33 px) and 47 px when nodding. With the **Bangs** material (new material model, tml / ds): 41 / 30 px shaking the head, 12 / 10 px nodding, 10 / 6 px left after a head turn stops, bake R² 0.18 → 0.27 / 0.21 → 0.49, 95% error 216 → 85 / 112 → 57 px. The skirt data was measured with the collision feature that has since been removed and needs testing again. Adding mass / wind groups to hair lowers the back hair's R² from 0.69 to 0.56, so the hair presets do not write those two groups.
- Obstacles: a body can carry obstacles (`obstacles`), circles or capsules following another mesh that keep particles outside; they take part in the reference simulation and baking, and export to runtime targets that play the live simulation (p2lrt, web). For now they can only be authored through MCP `simulation_put`; there is no editing on the canvas yet. The older mesh colliders (by margin) proved unreliable in testing and were removed; such colliders and collision weight groups in old projects are ignored on load.
- Not implemented yet: exporting wind and other force fields, rigid and soft bodies, baking into ordinary keys (dropping the generator).
- Looseness heat map checked by eye (tml, ds and one chibi set): on tml only the lower half of the skirt is loose; on ds the apron, the bow at the back of the waist and the hem are loose, while the puff sleeves, cuffs and sock tops are tight; on the chibi set the open coat is loose below the waistline and the straight trousers are tight.
- To be tested: how the skirt and hair of the example models look in the official runtime after export; how natural the hair and clothing simulations made by the model presets are.
